Crate vs playpen for an apartment dog — which one a small home can live with

By Chris Y. · One dog, one apartment, a tape measure and a chest girth chart Updated September 12, 2026

Buy the crate. In an apartment a crate takes about four square feet and folds to a few inches thick, while a 16-square-foot pen takes a quarter of a small bedroom and has nowhere to go when it is not in use — and for the job most people are actually buying for, which is a dog that settles while you are out, the crate is also the better tool.

The pen wins in exactly two situations, and they are worth knowing before you spend. A puppy under about four months cannot hold a bladder through a workday, so a pen with a defined toilet area is kinder than a crate it has to soil. And a dog recovering from surgery under restricted-movement instructions often needs somewhere to be awake rather than somewhere to sleep.

Everything else is arithmetic. Below are the two products this comparison actually comes down to: the best-documented crate on the market and the pen most people end up with, compared on the figures their makers publish.

How to shop for a crate or a playpen

Floor area, with the door open
A 36-inch crate is about 5.75 square feet and a hinged door adds roughly two more feet of clear space in front of it. MidWest's 16 square feet of play area is not a marketing figure — it is the pen's actual claim on the room, and it cannot be reduced.
What it does when you want the room back
Both fold. A wire crate goes flat to a few inches and slides behind furniture; an accordion pen folds to a bundle of panels that is long, heavy and awkward. In a one-bedroom this is the difference that decides it.
Height, and whether the dog can leave
A crate is closed. A pen is a wall, and a wall has a height — MidWest sells the same pen from 18 to 48 inches for exactly this reason. A determined 30 lb dog will clear a 24-inch pen from standing.
Which one the dog settles in
Crate training works because a closed den asks for sleep. A pen asks for wakefulness, which is right for a puppy learning to be alone and wrong for a dog you want quiet in a building with neighbors.

Questions people ask

Can I use a pen and a crate together?

Yes, and for a puppy it is the standard arrangement — the crate stands open inside the pen as the bed, and the rest of the pen is the toilet and play area. It takes the full 16 square feet, so it is a temporary layout in a small home rather than a permanent one.

How tall does a pen need to be?

Taller than the dog can clear from standing, which for a medium adult dog means 42 or 48 inches rather than 24. MidWest sells six heights for that reason, and the shorter ones are for puppies and small breeds.

Is a crate cruel for a full workday?

A crate is for sleeping and short absences, not for eight hours as a routine. Adult dogs that are properly crate trained sleep through most of a workday; puppies cannot, which is the real reason to own a pen for a few months.

What do I do with the pen afterwards?

This is the question worth asking before buying. A 48-inch pen folds to a long bundle of panels with no obvious home in a flat, and MidWest does not publish a folded dimension. A wire crate folds to a few inches and goes behind a wardrobe.

Also considered

  • Fabric playpens — Light, packable and no obstacle to a dog that wants out. Fine for a settled dog at a campsite; useless for the containment job a pen is bought to do.
