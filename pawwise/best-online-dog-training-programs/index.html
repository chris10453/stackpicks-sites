Online dog training is not one product. Three of the six below are recorded courses you buy once, one is an app subscription, one is a term-based school with instructor feedback, and one is a video library with a coaching club attached. They are not really competitors; they are answers to different questions about how much help you want and how often.

A note on what this page will not do. Nobody here can tell you a program will fix a specific behavior, and no program on this page publishes a study. What each one does publish is a format, an access length and a support arrangement, and those are the three things compared below. Serious aggression, biting and separation distress are veterinary behavior problems, not course material.

How to shop for an online dog training program

What the format actually is
Video, written lessons with photographs, a phone app or a term-based class are four different experiences. Brain Training for Dogs is video plus step-by-step pictures; Fenzi is lectures with assignments; Dogo is an app. Pick the one you will actually open on a Tuesday evening.
How long you keep access
One-time purchase, lifetime membership, per-term enrollment and monthly subscription all appear below. Fenzi gives a library pass for one year from enrollment; Baxter & Bella sells a lifetime membership; an app stops when you stop paying.
Whether a human ever sees your dog
This is the biggest difference in the category. Fenzi's Gold level includes instructor feedback on submitted video; Baxter & Bella publishes live one-to-one support; a recorded course gives you a forum and an email address.
What the vendor publishes about its trainer
Adrienne Farricelli publishes a CPDT-KA certification. Doggy Dan publishes certification in a named method and a list of trainers he has studied under. Fenzi publishes its whole instructor roster. Two here name nobody at all. Dogo offers only the phrase "certified experts", and Baxter & Bella publishes no credentials for its founder.
Whether the method is reward-based
The American Veterinary Society of Animal Behavior's position statement on humane dog training recommends reward-based methods and advises against tools and techniques that work through fear or discomfort. Every program on this page presents itself as reward-based; that is a floor, not a distinction.

Judge it as a structured library you buy once, with a forum and an email route to the trainer, and a stated 60-day window if it is not for you. Judge it as personal coaching and you will be disappointed, because it does not claim to be that.

Questions people ask

Can an online program really train a dog?

It can teach you a method and give you a plan, which for everyday problems — pulling, jumping, barking at the door, recall — is most of the job. What it cannot do is watch your dog. Fenzi's Gold tier and Baxter & Bella's live support are the two answers on this page to that limitation.

One-time purchase or subscription?

One-time if you will dip back in over years, subscription if you want ongoing coaching. Brain Training for Dogs and Baxter & Bella are bought once; Dogo and Doggy Dan's coaching club are ongoing; Fenzi is per term with a one-year library pass.

Are these programs humane?

All six present themselves as reward-based, and the AVSAB position statement on humane dog training recommends exactly that. The statement is linked in the sources; read it before buying anything that promises fast results through correction.

My dog has bitten someone. Is a course the answer?

No. Aggression, resource guarding and severe separation distress are veterinary behavior problems. Start with your veterinarian, who can rule out pain and refer you to a board-certified veterinary behaviorist. No program on this page is a substitute for that.

How long should I expect to spend on this?

Five to fifteen minutes a day, most days, is the shape that works. Dogo is built around that length; Fenzi's three-week and six-week terms assume homework between sessions; a recorded course will sit unopened without a habit around it.

Also considered

  • Free video platforms — There is excellent free dog training online and no way for a beginner to tell it from the rest. The reason to pay for any of the above is a sequence and somebody accountable for it, which a search result does not give you.
  • Programs selling shock or prong collar methods — Excluded on method. The AVSAB position statement recommends reward-based training and advises against techniques that work through fear, pain or discomfort.
  • Programs whose vendor page could not be read — Several well-known programs answered no automated request when checked. If a vendor's own claims could not be quoted from the vendor's own page, the program is not on this page, because every other row here is built from those quotes.
