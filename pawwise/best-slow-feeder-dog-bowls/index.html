A slow feeder is a bowl with obstacles in it. That is the whole product, and the honest way to compare five of them is by what they hold, how much counter or floor they take, and what stops the dog shoving the bowl across the room — because a slow feeder that slides is just a normal bowl with a longer chase.

One warning before the rows: every maker on this page frames its bowl around bloat, and not one of them publishes a study behind that. Rapid eating is a reasonable thing to slow down. Treat a slow feeder as a way to make a meal last ten minutes instead of ninety seconds, and treat a deep-chested dog's bloat risk as a conversation with a veterinarian.

How to shop for a slow-feeder bowl

Published capacity in cups
A slow feeder that holds less than the meal means feeding twice, which defeats it. The five here run from an unpublished figure to six cups, and a 70 lb dog on three cups a sitting rules out half of them.
The footprint it takes
Slow feeders are wide, because the maze needs area the food would otherwise get as depth. The 6-cup Neater Slow Feeder is 15.5 by 10.25 inches — larger than a dinner plate — and only two of the five bowls here have a published footprint — both of them Neater Pets'.
What stops it moving
A dog pushing at an obstacle course moves the bowl. Rubber rings, non-skid feet and a weighted non-tip shape are the three answers; a bowl with none of them ends up under the sofa with the kibble.
How it cleans
Wet food dries into a maze and stays there. Top-rack dishwasher safety is the difference between a bowl you use and a bowl you resent, and three of the five bowls here are published as dishwasher safe — the other two are the ones you will be brushing by hand.
Whether it suits the dog's face
Flat-faced dogs cannot reach into a narrow channel. Neater Pets says its wide cups are comfortable for them; Outward Hound sells a specific pattern for flat-faced breeds. Everything else here assumes a muzzle.

Questions people ask

Do slow feeders actually prevent bloat?

No maker on this page publishes a study saying so, and several of them frame the product around bloat anyway. What a slow feeder demonstrably does is stretch a meal — Outward Hound claims up to ten times longer at the bowl. If your dog is a deep-chested breed and bloat is the worry, that is a veterinary conversation, not a bowl purchase.

What size slow feeder do I need?

Match the capacity to one meal, not one day. A 25 lb dog eating about a cup per meal is fine on a medium Fun Feeder at 2 cups; a 70 lb dog on three cups needs the 6-cup Neater, the Extra-Large stainless bowl at 5 cups, or two sittings.

How do I clean the narrow channels?

Top rack of the dishwasher for the Fun Feeder, dishwasher for the stainless Non-Tip and the LickiMat, and a brush for everything else. Wet food dries into molded plastic hardest; the JW's three wide indentations are the easiest shape here to do by hand.

My dog just pushes the bowl around the kitchen.

That is the base failing, not the maze. A molded rubber bottom like the JW's TPR base or the removable ring on the stainless Neater is what stops it; a flat plastic disc on tile will always travel.

Is a lick mat better than a slow-feeder bowl?

It is better at a different job. A mat handles wet food, toppers and enrichment; a bowl handles kibble. At roughly the same money as the cheapest bowl here, most households end up with one of each.

Also considered

  • Stainless slow-feed inserts sold for feeder stands — Neater Pets sells several bowls designed to drop into its own Neater Feeder stands rather than to stand alone. They are good bowls in the wrong shape for a kitchen floor, and buying one without the stand is a common mistake.
  • Puzzle feeders sold as slow feeders — Sliding-tile and rotating-lid puzzles are enrichment toys that happen to hold food. They are worth owning and they are not a daily bowl — most need supervision and none hold a full meal.
  • Ceramic maze bowls — Attractive, heavy enough to stay put, and no US maker found publishes a capacity or a dimension for one. A bowl that can chip in a way plastic and stainless cannot deserves a published spec sheet before it gets a recommendation.
  • Elevated slow feeders marketed for large breeds — Raising a bowl is a separate decision with its own veterinary debate, and bundling it into a slow feeder hides the choice. The Neater Slow Feeder's optional leg extensions are the honest version — the height is an add-on you opt into.
