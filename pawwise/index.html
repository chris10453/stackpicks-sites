Dog gear for a small home — a buying guide that starts with the measurement

The dog aisle sells adjectives. Sturdy, chew-proof, no-pull, indestructible — five words that appear on every product in a category and tell you nothing about whether the thing fits your dog, your doorway or your floor.

Every guide here starts from a number the manufacturer published, and says so when the manufacturer published nothing. Two crate brands on this site name their crates by length and print no width at all. One bed brand scores its own beds out of ten for chewing and gives its cushioned bed a 6. Those two facts are more useful than a hundred adjectives.

Start with the measurement

Most returns in this category are fit failures rather than taste failures. A crate two inches narrower than the corner it was bought for. A harness whose size chart was measured in a different place from the tape. A slow feeder the size of a serving platter in a galley kitchen. Take three numbers before you shop — your dog's chest girth, the floor rectangle you can give up, and the height of the dog sitting — and most of the wrong purchases disappear.

How PawWise picks

Every specification on this site is quoted from the manufacturer's own published page or spec sheet, and every guide lists those sources at the bottom so you can check them. Where a brand publishes nothing, the guide prints "Not published" rather than borrowing a figure from a retailer and presenting it as the maker's. Where a maker and a retail page disagree — and they do — both figures are printed and attributed.

We never print prices or star ratings. A price typed into a page is wrong within a week; the buttons send you to the retailer to see today's figure. Awards are written around a buyer — best for a living room, best for a dog that also digs — because a single "best" is useless when the constraint is your floor plan.

Every retail link on this site is opened and checked for a live buy box before a guide publishes. Several products that would otherwise have been recommended are named in the rejects instead, with the reason and the date.
