A crate in a one-bedroom is a furniture decision before it is a training decision. The number that decides it is not the crate length printed on the box; it is the rectangle the crate occupies with its door open, in the only place the room has for it.

Three things separate the six crates below: what the maker publishes about the footprint, whether the door swings into the room or rolls up over the crate, and what the thing does on the days you need the floor back. Three of the six — two MidWest crates and the Petmate — publish nothing at all about width and height on their own product pages, and that is written into their rows.

How to shop for a dog crate for a small apartment

The footprint, not the crate length
Crates are named by length — a "36-inch" crate. The width is what a hallway or a bedroom wall actually rations, and it runs from 18 to 32 inches across this page for crates sold under nearly the same name.
Where the door goes when it is open
A hinged 36-inch door needs about two more feet of clear floor in front of it, every time. A roll-up door stows over the crate and needs none, which in a small room is worth more than any other feature on this page.
Interior inches, published by the maker
A dog needs to stand without ducking and turn without folding. Exterior dimensions include the frame, the pan lip and the door hardware, so an exterior figure overstates the space by two to three inches in each direction.
What it does when it is not in use
A wire crate folds to a few inches thick. A furniture crate never gets smaller and never will, which is the trade you make for a crate that can hold a lamp.
Whether the maker publishes anything at all
Two brands here name the crate by its length and publish no width or height on their own product page. That is a disclosure problem, and where it happens the row below says so rather than borrowing a number from somewhere else.

Questions people ask

What size crate does my dog need?

Measure the dog nose to tail base and add about 4 inches, then measure floor to the top of the head sitting and add about 3 inches, and buy the smallest crate whose interior beats both. Interior is the word that matters — the Petmate 28-inch has a 24.25 inch interior, roughly four inches less than its name suggests.

How much floor does a crate really take?

The crate plus its open door. A 36-inch wire crate with a hinged front door needs about 36 by 23 inches for the crate and roughly two more feet in front of it, so the honest figure is closer to 36 by 47 inches. The Ovation's roll-up door removes the second number entirely.

Is a furniture crate worth roughly double a wire crate?

If the crate has to stand in the room you entertain in, probably. The New Age Pet crates add a solid usable top and a 10-year limited warranty; a wire crate adds a divider panel and folds to a few inches thick. They are answers to different constraints, not the same crate at two prices.

Which crate is quietest for an apartment?

The plastic Ultra Vari Kennel, because solid walls and a single latch have nothing to rattle. A wire crate with a plastic pan is the noisiest; the Evolv's nylon-coated mesh sits between the two.
