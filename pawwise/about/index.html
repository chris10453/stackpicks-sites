About PawWise

PawWise is an independent buying guide for people who live with a dog in a home that is not big enough for dog gear to be ugly or oversized. I am Chris Y., and I write every guide here. One dog, one apartment, one hallway where the crate has to go.

The guides compare the things a product page hides: the interior of a crate rather than its exterior, the girth range a harness is actually cut for, what a bed maker will commit to about chewing, how much floor a slow feeder takes. Those are the numbers that decide whether a thing works in your home, and they are the numbers most dog-gear roundups skip.

How a guide gets made

Every specification on this site comes from the manufacturer's own published page, manual or spec sheet, and each guide lists its sources at the bottom so you can check them. Where a brand does not publish a number, the guide says "Not published by that brand" instead of guessing. Where a maker's figure and a retail page disagree, both are printed with attribution rather than the tidier one being chosen.

Every retail link is opened and checked for a live buy box before a guide publishes, and the date of that check is written into the page. Products that failed the check are named in the rejects with the reason — that is why a well-known crate brand and a well-known bed brand appear as rejects on this site rather than as picks.

We do not accept payment for placement. Brands cannot see a guide before it publishes, cannot buy an award and cannot change a ranking. We earn a commission when you buy through a link (see the affiliate disclosure), and that commission is broadly the same whichever pick you choose, which is exactly why the ranking can stay honest.

What we do not do

We do not print prices, because a price typed into a page is wrong within a week; the retailer's page is the only honest source for what something costs today. We do not print star ratings or review counts. And we never present desk research as hands-on testing — every guide on this site today is built from published specifications, manuals and welfare guidance, and each one says so in "How we picked".

Nothing on this site is veterinary advice or a behavior diagnosis. The training and barking pages point at the American Veterinary Society of Animal Behavior's guidance and at your own veterinarian, because a frightened dog and a noisy one need different help.
