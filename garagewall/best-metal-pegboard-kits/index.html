Two numbers decide a pegboard purchase, the hanging load and the hole pitch, and no manufacturer publishes both. Wall Control publishes loads and no pitch; Triton a pitch to the half inch and no load; OmniWall neither. Wall Control also argues that a single idealised laboratory rating is potentially misleading — which is why the "350 lbs per board" figure attached to competing steel boards is worth nothing.

If your tools are heavy and awkward rather than numerous, look at Triton's LocBoard instead: 18-gauge steel, square holes on 1-1/2 inch centers, and hooks that lock in so the hook does not come off the wall with the tool. You give up every round peg you own.

How to shop for metal pegboard

Does the brand publish a hanging load
One of six does. Wall Control publishes per-panel and per-hook recommendations; Triton says only "highest industry load ratings"; OmniWall says nothing at all.
Hole shape, and whether your hooks fit
Wall Control takes slotted hooks and quarter-inch pegs; LocBoard is square-hole only; DuraBoard is round quarter-inch on 1-inch centers. The wrong choice makes your hook collection scrap.
Gauge, and whether it needs furring strips
Wall Control runs 20-gauge, LocBoard 18-gauge in a 9/16-inch boxed section, OmniWall 18-gauge; thicker steel is stiffer between fixings, which stops a loaded board bowing. Wall Control's 3/4-inch return flange and the boxed LocBoard mount straight to the wall, where DuraBoard and hardboard need a spacer kit or battens.
What is actually in the kit
A bare panel pack and a kit with 24 hooks, two shelves and three bins are different purchases, and Triton is the only brand itemising its hook assortment.

Questions people ask

How much weight can metal pegboard hold?

Only one manufacturer will say. Wall Control recommends no more than 100 pounds a panel on drywall anchors, 200 into studs, 10 to 15 pounds on a hook engaging two slots and 2 per plastic bin; Triton and OmniWall publish no figure at all.

Is metal pegboard better than hardboard?

For a garage, yes — Wall Control's 20-gauge steel is described by its maker as over ten times stronger than conventional pegboard, and neither steel nor polypropylene absorbs moisture. Triton's own hardboard publishes no hanging-load number and needs wood framing behind it.

Does pegboard need furring strips or a spacer behind it?

It depends on the board. Wall Control's 3/4-inch return flange and Triton's 9/16-inch boxed LocBoard stand themselves off the wall; flat quarter-inch boards need either the DuraHook 70016 spacer kit or wood battens, or the hooks have nowhere to go.

Will my old pegboard hooks fit a new metal board?

On Wall Control and OmniWall, yes — both accept conventional quarter-inch pegs. On LocBoard, no: square holes at 1-1/2 inch centers take LocHook 50000-series hooks only, though DuraBoard uses the classic round pattern at 1 inch on center.
