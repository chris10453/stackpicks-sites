A cabinet is the only garage storage that hides things, which is why it is the piece people buy when the garage has to look finished rather than just work. It is also the piece with the weakest published numbers — the total-capacity figure is easy to find and the per-shelf figure, which is what actually decides whether your cabinet sags, usually is not.

One brand here contradicts itself on its own product page: the spec table for Gladiator's Full-Door Wall GearBox says 150 pounds total and 40 a shelf, and the feature text beside it says 410 pounds a cabinet and 55 a shelf. That is not a typo we are fixing quietly — it is printed below, because when a brand publishes two numbers you load to the smaller one.

How to shop for a garage storage cabinet

Pounds per shelf, not pounds per cabinet
A 450-pound cabinet with four shelves is a 75-pound shelf, because you cannot stack the load in a column. Every pick below carries the per-shelf figure where the maker publishes one.
Depth, because depth is what you lose
These run 12 to 24 inches deep. Against the side wall of a 12-foot bay, an 18-inch cabinet leaves room to open a car door and a 24-inch one does not.
Does it deal with a sloping floor
Garage slabs are poured with a slope toward the door. Gladiator publishes leveling legs, Seville publishes four leveling feet with rubber pads; a cabinet without them stands crooked and the doors stop latching.
Welded or flat-packed
NewAge ships the Bold sets fully welded, which is stiffer and heavier; the Gladiator and Seville cabinets arrive flat and go together in an evening. One is a delivery problem, the other is an evening.
A warranty with a number
Gladiator publishes ten years on these cabinets. Most of the category publishes nothing, and a cabinet is a ten-year purchase if it is anything.
Somewhere you can actually buy it
Full-height steel cabinets are freight items, and most of this category has no retail buy box at all — only third-party sellers with no featured offer. Five of the six picks here are bought from the maker, and each pick says so rather than sending you to a page with no price on it.

Questions people ask

How much weight can a garage storage cabinet hold?

Between 150 and 800 published pounds across this page. Gladiator's wall cabinet lists 150 pounds total, its floor cabinets 450, Seville's 36-inch cabinet 500 and its 60-inch cabinet 800. Per shelf the range is 40 to 150 pounds, which is the figure that matters.

How deep should a garage cabinet be?

18 inches for a side wall, 24 for a back wall. A 12-foot bay with a 6-foot car leaves about 3 feet each side, and a 24-inch cabinet takes two of those three feet — enough to stop a car door opening fully.

Are welded garage cabinets worth it over ready-to-assemble?

For stiffness and time, yes; for published numbers, not necessarily. NewAge's welded Bold sets publish 24-gauge steel and no per-shelf rating, while Gladiator's flat-packed cabinets publish 75 pounds a shelf and ten years of warranty. Welded cabinets also arrive on a pallet.

Do garage cabinets need to be anchored to the wall?

Tall cabinets should be. A 72-inch cabinet is a tip hazard when the top shelf is loaded and a lower door is open, and both Gladiator and Seville publish wall-bracket or anchoring options. The wall-hung GearBox transfers its whole 150-pound rating into the wall system by design.

Will a cabinet stand level on a garage floor?

Not without help. Garage slabs are poured with a slope toward the door for drainage, so any cabinet over five feet needs the leveling feet both Gladiator and Seville include. Without them the doors swing open on their own.

What is the difference between a garage cabinet and a locker?

Shelf layout, mostly. A cabinet is two to four horizontal shelves; a locker is one tall bay for rakes, brooms and a garment rod. Seville's wardrobe version of the UltraHD cabinet publishes 575 pounds total with 75 of that allotted to the hanging rod.

Also considered

  • Plastic resin storage cabinets — They survive damp better than powder-coated steel and publish shelf ratings around 25 to 75 pounds. For garden chemicals and hoses that is enough; for tools it is a quarter of what the steel cabinets here carry, and the doors sag in heat.
  • Wall-hung cabinets sold without a mounting system — Several look like the Gladiator wall box and ship with two keyhole slots and no bracket. A 150-pound cabinet hanging on two screws requires both to land in studs 16 inches apart, which is a coincidence rather than a plan.
  • Cabinet sets photographed as a wall but sold as pieces — Whole-garage photographs of matching cabinetry usually price a single base unit. Check the piece count in the product title — an eight-piece set and a one-piece cabinet look identical in a wide photograph.
  • Cabinets with no published capacity at all — Most of the first page of search results, including several 71-inch rolling metal cabinets from sellers with no manufacturer site. A cabinet whose maker will not publish a shelf rating has told you what it thinks the shelf will hold.
