Nobody here measures the same thing. Rubbermaid publishes pounds per rail; Gladiator per linear foot on tracks and per square foot on panels; Proslat, storeWALL and NewAge per square foot, at 75, 300 and 100. Those do not line up, so the comparable facts are the attachment ones: four of the seven include no mounting screws, and three lock you into their own hooks.

A rail and a panel are not the same purchase: a rail puts a strong horizontal line at one height, a panel puts a weaker hanging surface across the whole wall. Which you want depends on whether your problem is heavy tools or a lot of light ones — there is a zone map in how to plan a garage wall.

How to shop for a garage wall system

What the capacity is measured per
Per rail, per linear foot, per square foot and per hook are four different units, and a 1,750 lb rail and a 75 lb per-square-foot panel can carry similar loads across a wall.
The per-hook number, which is the real limit
Rubbermaid rates its own accessories at 25 to 50 pounds each. That, not the rail, decides whether the pressure washer hangs there.
Are the screws in the box
GearTrack and GearWall both say "Color Matched Screws sold separately", and storeWALL sells its InstallStrips separately. Proslat and NewAge include hardware.
Proprietary or cross-compatible hooks
Proslat, Gladiator and storeWALL are all closed systems. NewAge's 3-inch on-center slots take most standard slatwall accessories — the only escape hatch here.
Stud requirement
FastTrack must start and end mid-stud, Proslat installs on 16-inch centers, and FLEXIMOUNTS takes 16, 18 or 24 but refuses metal studs.
A warranty with a term
Rubbermaid and Proslat publish lifetime terms, Gladiator and storeWALL 10 years, and NewAge and FLEXIMOUNTS no term at all.

Questions people ask

How much weight can a garage wall track hold?

It depends what the brand measures — Rubbermaid publishes 1,750 pounds per 48-inch rail but 25 to 50 per hook; Gladiator 75 per linear foot and 50 per square foot; Proslat 75 a square foot, NewAge 100, storeWALL 300 with its own strips.

Is slatwall or a rail better for a garage?

A rail gives one very strong horizontal line for heavy items at far less per wall; a panel gives a weaker hanging surface everywhere, which suits a lot of light tools. Pressure washer and two ladders, buy a rail; thirty garden tools, buy panels.

Do these systems come with screws?

Three of the six do not. Gladiator says "Color Matched Screws sold separately" on the GearTrack channels, storeWALL sells InstallStrips separately, and FastTrack hardware is its own part number; Proslat, NewAge and FLEXIMOUNTS include wood-stud hardware.

Can I use any slatwall hooks on any slatwall?

Usually not — Proslat's FAQ says Proslat accessories, and storeWALL uses a patented profile. NewAge is the exception, with 3-inch on-center slots and stated compatibility with most standard slatwall accessories.
