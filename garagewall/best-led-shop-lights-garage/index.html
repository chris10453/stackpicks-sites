Garage lighting is arithmetic, not opinion. A two-car garage is about 400 square feet, a bright working shop wants roughly 50 lumens per square foot, and 400 × 50 is 20,000 lumens. Three Barrina BAL50s get there. Six Sunco wraparounds get there. One bulb in a ceiling fixture, which is what most garages have, gives you about 800.

The other number that decides the purchase is how many fixtures link off one plug: eight for the Sunco wraparound, five for the Barrina BAL50, four for the Sunco white-endcap six-pack, three for the Barrina BS84. Divide your target lumens by the fixture, then check the run limit — that is the whole decision, and it is why the brightest fixture is not always the right one.

How to shop for a garage shop light

Published lumens, not "equivalent" watts
"40W=300W" tells you nothing about the room. Every fixture here carries the lumen figure its maker publishes: 3,500 to 12,000 per fixture.
How many link on one circuit
A run limit of three versus eight changes how many outlets you need in the ceiling. Sunco publishes eight for the wraparound; Barrina publishes five for the BAL50 and three for the BS84.
Color temperature
5000 K reads as daylight and suits color-matching and detail work; 6500 K is bluer and looks brighter for the same lumens. Both are sold in this category and the difference is visible.
Plug-in or hardwired
A corded fixture with a pull chain needs a ceiling outlet and takes ten minutes. A hardwired fixture needs the circuit opened. Two of these ship both ways.
A warranty with a number, and a damp rating
Sunco publishes seven years and a damp rating on all three fixtures here, and Feit publishes 25,000 rated hours and a five-year warranty. An unheated garage cycles through 100 degrees of temperature swing a year, and that is what kills drivers.
Somewhere you can actually buy it

Questions people ask

How many lumens do I need to light a two-car garage?

About 20,000. A two-car garage is roughly 400 square feet and a working shop wants around 50 lumens per square foot, which is three Barrina BAL50s at 7,000 lumens each, two Feit high-output fixtures at 12,000, or six Sunco wraparounds at 3,500.

Is 5000 K or 6500 K better for a garage?

5000 K for most people. It reads as daylight and shows paint and wiring colors honestly; 6500 K is bluer and appears brighter at the same lumen count, which some find harsh over a bench. Both are stocked by every brand here, and the Barrina BS84 is sold in each.

How many LED shop lights can I link together?

Between three and eight, depending on the fixture. Sunco publishes eight for the wraparound and four for the corded white-endcap six-pack, Barrina five for the BAL50 and three for the 84-watt BS84. Exceeding the published limit overloads the first fixture's connector, not just the circuit.

Do LED shop lights work in a cold garage?

Better than fluorescent tubes, which dim and flicker below freezing. The fixtures here are damp-location rated; for genuinely wet bays a vapor-tight fixture with an IP rating is the right category instead.

Should garage lights be plug-in or hardwired?

Plug-in if you have a ceiling outlet, hardwired if you want no cords visible. The Sunco motion fixture and both Barrinas ship with cords; the Sunco wraparound is a surface-mount hardwired fixture at 120 to 277 volts.

Where should shop lights go over a workbench?

In front of where you stand, not directly overhead, so your body does not cast the shadow onto the work. Two 4-foot fixtures running parallel to the bench and about 18 inches in front of its edge light the work rather than your shoulders.

Also considered

  • Fixtures sold by "equivalent wattage" with no lumen figure — A large share of this category advertises "60W=500W" and never publishes an actual lumen number. Without lumens you cannot size a room, and the absence is usually deliberate.
  • UFO high-bay lights for a residential garage — The 200 to 300-watt high-bay fixtures that appear in the same search results publish 30,000 to 45,000 lumens and are designed for warehouse ceilings of 15 feet and up. Hung at nine feet, they produce glare rather than light.
  • Fluorescent tube fixtures and LED tube retrofits — A fluorescent shop light dims in the cold and needs ballast and tube replacements; retrofit tubes add a compatibility question about the ballast. Integrated LED fixtures at 25,000 rated hours have no consumable parts.
  • Grow-light fixtures sold in the same lists — They appear in shop-light searches because the tubes look identical, but the output is tuned for plants rather than eyes. Color rendering under one is poor enough to make paint matching impossible.
