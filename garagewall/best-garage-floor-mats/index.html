There are two products in this category wearing one name. A containment mat has a raised edge and holds the slush, salt and mud that comes off a car until you squeegee it out. A floor mat is absorbent carpet with a waterproof backing that soaks up drips and dries out. One is a tray, the other is a towel, and buying the wrong one is the most common mistake here.

This is a January purchase for a reason: road salt tracked into a heated garage is what stains a slab and eats the bottom of a door. Three brands publish a specification page worth reading — AutoFloorGuard, Drymate and G-Floor — and everything below comes from them.

How to shop for a garage floor mat

Containment edge or absorbent surface
A containment mat's raised edge keeps liquid in and needs emptying; an absorbent mat wicks and dries. Snow-belt garages want containment, oil drips want absorbent.
Published size against your vehicle
A full-size truck is roughly 19 feet long. AutoFloorGuard publishes 7'6" × 16', 7'6" × 18' and 8'6" × 20'; short-measuring by two feet puts the rear wheels on bare concrete.
What the material actually is
Drymate publishes 100% polyester with a bonded waterproof backing, G-Floor publishes 100% solid polyvinyl with no fillers or layers, AutoFloorGuard heavy polyester scrim. Everyone else says "heavy duty".
How the seams are made
Heat-sealed and thermally welded seams hold water; stitched ones wick it through. Only one brand here publishes how its seams are made.
Whether it can stay down all year
A rolled vinyl floor is permanent and takes furniture and jacks. A containment mat is a winter tool that folds away in April, and a felt mat lives somewhere in between.

Questions people ask

What size garage floor mat do I need for a truck?

About 8 ft 6 in by 20 ft. AutoFloorGuard's three published sizes are 7'6" × 16', 7'6" × 18' and 8'6" × 20'; a crew-cab pickup is close to 19 feet long, so only the largest keeps all four wheels on the mat.

Do containment mats really hold melted snow?

The good ones do, and the seams are why. AutoFloorGuard publishes heat-sealed, thermally welded seams and a raised containment edge; the water pools in the middle and comes out with the included squeegee rather than running onto the slab.

Absorbent mat or containment mat for a snowy garage?

Containment. An absorbent mat holds a few gallons and then needs drying, which in January it never gets. Use absorbent mats for oil drips and walkways and a containment mat under the vehicle.

Will a garage mat damage the concrete underneath?

Only if moisture stays trapped under it. Every mat here publishes a waterproof backing, which keeps water off the slab, but a mat left down on a wet floor traps what is already there. Lift and dry the slab before it goes down.

Can garage floor mats be cut to fit?

Drymate publishes that its garage floor mat can be cut for a custom fit, and G-Floor rolls can be trimmed with a utility knife. Containment mats cannot — cutting one removes the raised edge that makes it a containment mat.

How do you clean a garage containment mat?

Squeegee out the standing water, then broom or vacuum dry dirt. AutoFloorGuard includes a squeegee and telescoping pole for exactly this, and the mat hoses down when the weather allows.

Also considered

  • Unbranded 7'9" × 18' PVC containment mats — They dominate the search results, quote a fabric weight in grams per square meter, and come from sellers with no product page, no warranty document and no company behind the number. The gram figure is the only specification any of them publishes.
  • Park Smart Clean Park mats — A long-standing name in this category sold through auto-parts retailers, left out because no manufacturer page could be found that publishes the mat's size, material or warranty — only retailer listings quoting a mil thickness.
  • Interlocking garage floor tiles — They handle a dropped tool better than vinyl and let water drain under them, which is fine outdoors and a problem in a garage where salt water gets under the tile and stays there. Rolled flooring has no seams for it to reach.
  • Epoxy floor coatings — The best-looking answer and the wrong one for most people. Epoxy needs a dry, ground, acid-etched slab and days of curing, it fails where moisture comes up through the concrete, and it does not come back up when you move.
