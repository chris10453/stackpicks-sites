How to build a home office in the corner of a room

Most home-office advice is written for a spare room. This site is for the other case: a corner of a bedroom, a studio flat, an alcove between a window and a door, where the binding constraint is almost never money and almost always inches.

Start with how to measure a small workspace. It takes ten minutes with a tape and it is the guide that decides every other purchase — how deep the desk can be, whether your screen will sit far enough away, whether the chair fits underneath, and whether the shelf above has to come down.

Every specification on this site comes from the manufacturer's own product page, and every source is linked at the bottom of the guide that uses it. Where a maker publishes nothing, the table says "not published" rather than an invented number. No prices are printed here, because retailers change them hourly and a stale figure is worse than none.

How these guides are made

Every figure is quoted from the manufacturer's own product page or dimensioned drawing. Nothing is taken from a retailer's copy, and where two of a maker's own pages disagree — as Mount-It!'s do about one monitor arm's reach — both figures are printed and the conservative one is used.

Products are compared against one situation: a desk between 15 and 24 inches deep in a room where the floor in front of it is a walkway rather than an office. That is why depth is the first column in most of the tables here and price is nearly the last.

Price bands describe where a product sits relative to the others on its own page, checked against live retail pages on the date at the top of each guide. They are not a claim about what anything costs next week.
