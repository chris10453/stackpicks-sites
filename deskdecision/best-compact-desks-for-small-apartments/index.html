Almost everyone shops a compact desk by width, because width is what the product name says. Depth is what makes a room unwalkable: a 24-inch desk against a bedroom wall leaves nine fewer inches of floor in front of it than a 15-inch one, and where the bed is four feet away that is the difference between a workspace and an obstacle.

Depth has a cost worth naming: a shallow desk cannot put a monitor at arm's length on its own, which is why nearly everyone who buys one fits a monitor arm. Want the depth in the desk instead? The MICKE at 19⅝ inches and the VIVO mobile desk at 22. Every dimension below is quoted from the maker's own page.

How to shop for a compact desk

Published depth, before anything else
The range here runs from 15⅜ to 24 inches. That nine-inch spread is floor in a room that has none.
Clear knee height and knee width
IKEA publishes 28⅜ inches of clearance under the KALLAX top. Most makers publish nothing, so the crossbar that ruins the knee space is a surprise you get on assembly day.
A published load rating
Two monitors, a dock and a laptop is 40 to 60 lb. Mount-It! rates the MI-8035 at 154 lb and VIVO the mobile desk at 88; IKEA and Branch publish no figure for these models at all.
Whether the desk can stop being a desk
In a studio the furniture shares the room with the evening. A wall leaf folds flat; a desk on casters leaves the room. A fixed slab does neither.
Where the cable run goes
MICKE has a cable outlet in its back panel and KALLAX hides the run behind the shelf leg. On the flat-topped desks it is on you, and an under-desk tray is the fix.

Questions people ask

How deep does a desk need to be?

Deep enough to put the screen roughly an arm's length away — CCOHS puts the eye's resting point of accommodation at about 80 cm, or 31.5 inches. On a factory stand that wants around 24 inches of desk; below that a monitor arm does the job instead.

Will a chair with arms fit under these?

IKEA publishes 28⅜ inches of clear height under the KALLAX top, which takes most task chairs with the arms lowered. The other five publish no clearance figure, so measure your chair's arm height against the desk height minus the top thickness.

Is a 15-inch-deep desk actually usable?

For a laptop, a keyboard and a mouse, yes — a full-size keyboard is about 6 inches deep, a laptop about 9. It stops working the moment a monitor sits on the desk rather than over it.

Can I put a monitor arm on any of these?

On the flat-topped ones, yes, if the top thickness suits the clamp — Mount-It! publishes a 0.6-inch top for the MI-8035, inside every clamp range in our arm guide. The wall-mounted leaf is the exception: its 33 lb rating includes the arm.

Standing desk or a converter on a normal desk?

A converter is cheaper and fits the desk you own, but it adds 15 to 26 inches of depth on the desk and never gives it back. On a shallow desk that is usually the wrong trade. The full comparison is in standing desk versus desk converter.

How much floor does the desk itself need?

Add about 30 inches in front for a chair you can push back and sit in. A 15⅜-inch desk claims about 46 inches of room depth; a 24-inch desk claims about 54.

Also considered

  • Corner and L-shaped desks sold as space-saving — An L-desk claims two walls and the corner between them — in a small room, the only place a bed or sofa can go.
  • Desks whose only published dimension is the top — Several popular flat-pack desks publish a width and a depth and nothing about height, clearance or load — and height decides whether your chair works.
  • Folding card-table style desks — Right idea, but the tops flex under a monitor arm and almost none publish a load rating. The wall leaf does the same job with a stated 33 lb limit.
  • Ladder and leaning desks — The shelves above the worktop are the selling point and the problem: they take the space a monitor needs and force the screen forward.
  • Sauder and Bush Furniture desks — Both make sensible compact desks and neither publishes dimensions in a form we could read from their own public pages, so nothing they sell could be compared here on equal terms.
