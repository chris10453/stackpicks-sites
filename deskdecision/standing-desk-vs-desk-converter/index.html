Standing desk vs desk converter for a small room (2026)

By Chris Y. · Founder & editor Updated September 12, 2026

The short answer: buy the converter if your desk is 24 inches deep or more and you like it, and replace the desk if it is shallower than that. A converter sits on top of the desk, so it inherits whatever depth the desk already has and then spends some of it on a keyboard tray. On a shallow desk that is the wrong trade, and a compact electric desk costs about the same.

The two solve the same problem from opposite ends. A converter is a riser you drop on the worktop you own — no assembly, no disposal, and it comes off when you move. A standing desk replaces the furniture and gives a flat top at every height.

In a small room a third factor decides it more often than either of those: what the thing looks like when it is down. A converter at its lowest setting still has a keyboard tray and a frame on the desk. A standing desk at its lowest setting is a desk.

How to shop for a sit-stand setup

What it does to depth
A converter's keyboard tray sits over the desk edge, so usable depth behind it shrinks. The VIVO K Series top is 15.7 inches deep, the Mount-It! MI-7957 is 18.
The low position, not the high one
You spend most of the day at the low setting. A converter's lowest is 4.5 inches on the VIVO and 5 on the Mount-It! — a permanent step. A standing desk's low position is a flat top at 28.7 inches.
Published weight capacity
Both converters publish 33 lb for the desktop and about 4 for the keyboard tray. A 27-inch monitor on an arm exceeds that, which most people discover after buying.
What it costs against a whole desk
A compact electric standing desk here costs less than one of the two converters. Once that is true, the converter's only argument is the desk you already own.

Questions people ask

Is a converter cheaper than a standing desk?

Not reliably. On Mount-It!'s own store the converter costs more than the 40-inch electric desk, and the desk publishes 154 lb of capacity against 33. Check both from the same brand.

How much desk does a converter take away?

All of the footprint it stands on, permanently. The VIVO base covers about 25.6 by 15.7 inches, the Mount-It! 15.75 by 19.75 — a third to a half of a 40 by 24-inch desk.

How high should the desk be when I stand?

CCOHS says the platform sits at roughly your standing elbow height, forearms parallel to the floor and elbows near 90 degrees. Measure floor to elbow standing and compare: 46.5 inches on the Mount-It! desk, 20 inches of lift above your desk on the VIVO.

How long should I stand for?

CCOHS says recommendations vary from 5 minutes in every hour to switching every 20, 30, 45 or 60 minutes, and that the key factor is alternating rather than the schedule.

Can I put a monitor arm on a converter?

Usually, on the rear edge of the upper platform — but both converters publish 33 lb for the whole desktop, and a 20 lb arm plus a 12 lb monitor is already at the limit. Clamp the arm to the desk itself instead.
