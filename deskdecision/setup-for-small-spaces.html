SETUP GUIDE

Measure first. Buy second.

A small workspace gets expensive when every purchase solves a different problem. Use this order to avoid buying a desk, keyboard, and organizer that still do not work together.

1. Draw the footprint

Measure wall-to-wall width, usable depth, chair clearance, and the nearest outlet. Leave room to move the chair without hitting a bed, cabinet, or door. Record the measurements in inches before opening a product page.

2. Name the bottleneck

If the work surface is too small, start with the desk. If you switch devices, start with input. If the floor is covered in cords, start with routing. Do not let a visually attractive accessory distract from the constraint.

3. Verify the boring details

Check dimensions, mounting hardware, return terms, seller identity, compatibility, and current price on the live product page. Product listings change. Our snapshot is a research aid, not a promise.

4. Keep one thing adjustable

Fixed-height desks, wall rails, and wired power arrangements can be perfectly workable, but they reduce flexibility. If the room is changing, preserve adjustability somewhere in the setup rather than optimizing every item for one exact layout.
