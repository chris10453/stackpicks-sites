A tire that is 6 PSI low is invisible. It does not look flat, the car does not pull, and the only symptoms are a fuel bill and a tire that wears out at the edges a year early. Spend as little as possible and the AstroAI L7 does the top-up job for a fraction of the price; already own cordless tools and the Milwaukee M12 compact inflator is the fastest thing here on a published number.

Two things separate a useful inflator from a toy: an auto shut-off that stops at the pressure you set, and either a published duty cycle or a published per-tire time so you can work out whether it will do four tires without a rest. Everything below is judged on the manufacturers' own figures, linked at the bottom.

How to shop for a portable tire inflator

Auto shut-off at a set pressure
Without it you are watching a gauge and feathering a trigger in the dark. Every pick here except the VIAIR sets a target PSI and stops.
Duty cycle, not fill speed
Four tires is four times the work of one, and small inflators overheat. VIAIR publishes 25 minutes at 30 PSI for the 88P; the cordless units publish a per-tire figure instead, which is the number to hold against your own four-tire job.
Power source that matches the emergency
A cordless unit works when the car will not start. A 12V unit never runs out mid-fill but needs the engine on. A tool-battery unit is free if you already own the platform and pointless if you do not.
Maximum pressure against your actual tires
A passenger car runs 30 to 36 PSI, a light truck 45 to 60, a trailer up to 80. Anything rated to 120 PSI covers all of it, so the 150 and 160 PSI numbers on the box matter less than whether the pump still moves air near the top of its range.
A hose and chuck that reach the valve
Short hoses fight low-profile wheels and valve stems angled into the rim. Threaded chucks stay on; push-on chucks leak as you fit them.
A display you can read at night
Flat tires happen at night, on a shoulder, in the rain. An illuminated gauge and a work light are not extras in this category.

  1. Nothing on this page has been run against a tire by us yet. This is a specification comparison: every figure above is quoted from the manufacturer's own product page or manual, and those pages are linked in Sources at the bottom.

Questions people ask

What pressure should my tires actually be?

The number on the sticker in the driver's door jamb, not the number molded into the sidewall — that one is the tire's maximum, usually 44 to 51 PSI. Most passenger cars sit between 30 and 36 PSI cold, and every 10 degrees Fahrenheit of temperature drop takes about 1 PSI out of a tire.

How long does it take to fill a flat tire?

About 6 minutes on the Slime by its own published figure. A top-up from 28 to 35 PSI is under 1 minute on the Milwaukee 2475-20 and under 1.5 minutes on the AstroAI L7. Anything claiming 20 seconds is measuring a bicycle tire.

Cordless or 12V?

Cordless if the inflator lives in the car, because it works when the engine will not start. 12V or mains if it lives in the garage for monthly checks, because there is no battery to age. The full head-to-head is in the cordless versus 12V guide linked below.

Do these overheat?

Compact cordless units get hot doing four tires from flat, and most publish no duty cycle at all. VIAIR publishes 25 minutes at 30 PSI for the 88P, which is why it is the pick for anyone airing up truck tires regularly.

Also considered

  • Airmoto and similar pocket inflators — They fill one tire from flat and then need a rest and a recharge. Fine as a bicycle pump, not enough for a car at the roadside.
  • Socket compressors without auto shut-off — A gauge you have to watch is a tire you will over-inflate. Auto shut-off is the cheapest safety feature in this category, and every pick here has one except the VIAIR, which compensates with a real duty cycle.
  • Inflators with a 40 PSI ceiling — They cannot fill a compact spare, which is usually 60 PSI — the one tire you are most likely to have to inflate from flat.
  • Combination jump-starter and inflator units — Two tools sharing one battery, and the inflator side is usually the compromised half. Buy a jump pack and an inflator separately.
  • AstroAI AIRUN H — 12V-only, and AstroAI publishes no fill time for it. The dual-power AstroAI above does the same job, adds a mains option, and costs about the same.
