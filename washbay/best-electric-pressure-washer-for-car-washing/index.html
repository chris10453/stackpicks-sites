For a car, flow does more work than pressure. Rinsing a roof and running a foam cannon are both flow jobs, and almost every cannon maker publishes a minimum in GPM rather than PSI. Past about 2,000 PSI you are adding risk to trim, badges and old clear coat without cleaning the car any faster.

The trap in this aisle is the number on the carton. "Max PSI" is measured at a moment, not during work; the figure to compare is the rated or certified one — 2,030 PSI certified rated on the Sun Joe SPX3000, 1,900 rated PSI on the Westinghouse. Every specification below is the manufacturer's own, and the sources are linked at the bottom.

How to shop for an electric pressure washer

Rated PSI, not maximum PSI
Maximum is a peak; rated is what the machine holds while it works. Sun Joe publishes 2,030 PSI certified rated against 2,500 maximum, and Westinghouse publishes 1,900 rated against 2,300. Compare like with like.
Rated GPM
Flow rinses, and flow is what a foam cannon needs — most ask 1.1 to 1.4 GPM minimum. The washers here run 1.2 to 1.4 GPM rated, which is the band a driveway wash lives in.
Weight and height, because storage decides use
The Westinghouse is 19 lb and 16.5 inches tall; the Kärcher K1700 is 20.7 lb. A washer you can carry out with one hand gets used on a Saturday morning; a 40-pound trolley does not.
Hose length before you have to move the machine
AR Blue Clean ships 30 feet on a reel, Westinghouse 25 feet, Sun Joe and Kärcher 20. A sedan on a driveway needs about 25 feet to get all the way round without dragging the unit twice.
How the soap gets applied
Onboard tanks are convenient and weak; a foam cannon on the wand is the real answer. Sun Joe carries two 0.9 L tanks, Kärcher half a gallon, Westinghouse 20 oz — useful for a pre-soak, not a substitute for a cannon.
Total Stop System on the trigger
TSS shuts the pump down when the trigger is released instead of letting it cycle against a closed valve. Sun Joe and AR Blue Clean both name it; it is the single feature that most affects how long a cheap pump survives.

Questions people ask

How much PSI do I need to wash a car?

Between about 1,200 and 1,900 PSI at the nozzle is plenty, which every machine here can produce. Use the 25 or 40 degree tip, keep the nozzle a foot or more from the paint, and never use the 0 degree tip on a vehicle.

Is GPM or PSI more important for car washing?

GPM. Rinsing and foaming are both flow work — foam cannons publish minimums of 1.1 to 1.4 GPM and no minimum PSI that any of these machines fail. The washers here run 1.2 to 1.4 GPM rated.

What does certified rated PSI mean?

It means the figure was measured to a published industry test rather than taken at a peak. Sun Joe lists 2,030 PSI certified rated against a 2,500 maximum, and Westinghouse lists 1,900 rated against 2,300 maximum — roughly a 20 percent difference between the two ways of quoting the same machine.

Can I run hot water through an electric pressure washer?

Not unless the maker says so. Kärcher states a maximum inlet temperature of 104°F for the K1700, and that is typical for cold-water electrics. Warm water belongs in the foam cannon bottle, not in the inlet hose.

Will a pressure washer strip wax or damage paint?

At car-wash distances and with a fan tip, no. Damage comes from a 0 degree tip, from holding the nozzle inches from a badge or a rubber seal, or from blasting an existing chip. Ceramic sprays and waxes survive normal washing; they do not survive a pinpoint jet at close range.

Also considered

  • Gas pressure washers — Typically 2,800 to 4,200 PSI and far more flow than a car needs, with an engine to maintain. The right tool for a driveway and the wrong one for paint.
  • Marketplace washers advertising 4,000 PSI — No certified rating, no published flow under load, and a pressure figure a 15-amp household outlet cannot support. The number is the product.
  • Ryobi 2300 PSI brushless electric — The maker's own product page lists it as discontinued and publishes no flow rate, so there is no figure to match a foam cannon against.
  • Battery-powered portable washers — Useful for a bike or a muddy dog, not for a car — they run well under 800 PSI, carry their water with them, and stop when the pack does.
  • Washers sold only as a bundle with a surface cleaner — The surface cleaner is a driveway accessory that will never touch the car, and the bundle usually costs more than the machine and a foam cannon together.
