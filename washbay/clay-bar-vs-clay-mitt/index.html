Clay bar vs clay mitt: which one your car needs

By Chris Y. · Founder & editor Updated September 12, 2026

Both of these remove bonded fallout that a wash leaves behind. The difference is what each one costs you when it goes wrong. Drop a clay bar on the driveway and it is finished — you bin an 80 gram bar. Use a clay mitt on paint you are not going to polish afterwards and you may have to look at light marring until you do.

Adam's makes both of the products below and is unusually blunt about the second one: it states that the Clay Mitt is a medium grade that can leave light micro-marring, and that the mitt is ideal if you are planning a full machine polish afterwards. That single sentence is the whole decision.

Buy the Visco Clay Bar if the car is not getting polished. Buy the Clay Mitt if it is, or if you are doing several cars and the 20 to 40 published uses matter more than the last few percent of gentleness.

How to shop for a clay tool

What happens when you drop it
The bar is done: it has picked up grit that will scratch the next panel, and there is no way to clean it. The mitt is rinsed under a hose with your hands and carried on with. On a real driveway this happens more often than anyone plans for.
How long the job takes
A bar is worked in small sections with a folded face that has to be re-kneaded as it clogs. A mitt covers far more area per pass and does not need kneading, which is the difference between an afternoon and an hour on a mid-size car.
Whether it marks the paint
Adam's rates the Visco bar as safe on delicate clear coat and says it removes contamination without causing damage. For the mitt, the same maker warns of potential light micro-marring to be corrected by polishing. Two products, one brand, two honest and different claims.
Cost per car over time
The bar is a consumable with an 80 gram weight printed on it. The mitt publishes 20 to 40 uses. Over three years of twice-yearly decontamination the mitt is the cheaper tool by a wide margin — if it survives as claimed.

Questions people ask

Is a clay mitt more aggressive than a clay bar?

Adam's says its Clay Mitt is a medium grade, slightly more aggressive than its Visco Clay Bar, and that it could potentially leave light micro-marring. Both remove the same contamination; the mitt does it faster and leaves more behind to correct.

How many cars will one clay bar do?

Adam's publishes 80 g for the Visco bar, Chemical Guys publishes 100 g for its OG bar, and both are enough for several cars if the bar stays off the ground. In practice the limiting factor is not the clay running out, it is the bar being dropped.

Can I use a clay mitt on glass and wheels?

Adam's rates its Clay Mitt for paint, glass, wheels and chrome. Bonded fallout on a windshield is what causes the wiper judder people blame on their blades, and it responds to claying the same way paint does.

Do I need to polish after claying?

After a light-grade bar, usually not. After a medium-grade mitt, Adam's own wording assumes you will. Plan the polish before you choose the tool rather than the other way round.

What lubricant should I use?

A detail spray or a dedicated clay lubricant, generously, so the surface never goes dry under the tool. Adam's names its own Detail Spray as the preferred lubricant for the Clay Mitt. Claying dry is the mistake that causes real damage, with either tool.
