This is the only real decision in the inflator aisle. PSI ratings, work lights and the number of nozzle adapters are noise next to where the power comes from, because the power source decides whether the tool works on the specific bad day you bought it for. A cordless unit works with a dead car battery, in a parking lot, at night, ignition off. A 12V unit never has a flat battery of its own and never stops halfway up a truck tire.

The two failures are not symmetrical, which is why the answer depends on where the tool sits. A cordless inflator fails by being flat after six months of neglect — a calendar problem. A 12V inflator fails by needing an engine you cannot start — a physics problem. One of those you can fix with a reminder.

How to shop for a tire inflator

What fails, and when
A cordless inflator fails by being flat after six months in a hot trunk; a 12V inflator fails by needing an engine you cannot start. Pick the failure you can live with.
Current draw versus the socket
A 12V accessory socket is typically fused at 15 amps. A serious compressor like the VIAIR 88P draws around 20 amps and clamps directly to the battery terminals for that reason. Socket-powered units are limited by the fuse, not by the pump.
Four tires, not one
Compact cordless units publish per-tire times — 6 minutes from flat on the Slime, under 1.5 minutes for a top-up on the AstroAI L7 — and most publish no duty cycle. Corded units simply keep going.
Where it is stored
A trunk-stored tool has to hold charge unattended. Slime states four months of standby on the 40085's built-in battery, and that single spec is the whole argument for cordless.

Questions people ask

Can a 12V inflator drain my car battery?

Yes, if the engine is off. A compact compressor pulls 10 to 15 amps; twenty minutes of that on a battery already low is how a tire change turns into a jump start. Run the engine, or use a cordless unit.

How often do cordless inflators need charging?

Slime publishes a four-month standby charge on the 40085. A sensible habit is topping it up when the clocks change, which is twice a year and also the right moment to check all four tire pressures.

Which is faster?

Tool-battery units win on published numbers: Milwaukee states under a minute to top off a car tire on the 2475-20, and the Slime publishes 6 minutes from flat. Corded units publish no fill time at all, so they are compared on duty cycle instead.

Do I need 150 PSI?

Only for trailer or LT truck tires. A passenger car sits at 30 to 36 PSI and a compact spare at about 60. What matters is whether the pump still moves air near the top of its range, not the number printed on the box.

Can I keep a cordless inflator in a hot car?

Lithium cells age faster in heat, so a trunk in Florida summer is hard on them. Keep it in the cabin if you can, and expect a battery inflator to need replacing sooner than a corded compressor, which has no cells to degrade at all.
