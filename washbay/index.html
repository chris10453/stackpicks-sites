The car care guide for people who wash at home

Most car-care advice is written either for a detailing shop with a heated bay and a two-stage polisher, or by someone who has never coiled a hose in the dark. This site is for the third case: a driveway, a garden hose, two buckets and about an hour on a Saturday.

Start with the best car wash kit for beginners — it is the guide that decides whether your paint still looks good in five years. If you already wash properly, the vacuum, inflator and headlight guides below cover the three jobs that come next.

Every specification on this site comes from the manufacturer's own product page, and every source is linked at the bottom of the guide that uses it. No prices are printed here, because retailers change them hourly and a stale number is worse than none.

What this site will and will not print

  • 25 guides live, each with its sources linked at the bottom.
  • 0 prices typed by hand. Every product carries a band from $ to $$$$ and a link to check today's figure at the retailer.
  • No star scores, no invented head-to-heads. Nothing here has been timed or assembled by us yet, and every guide says so in its "How we picked" section.
  • Numbers come from the manufacturer. Air flow in CFM, maximum working pressure in PSI, towel weight in gsm, duty cycle in minutes — taken from the brand's own product page or manual. Where a brand publishes nothing, the table shows a dash rather than a borrowed figure.

How these guides are made

Each guide names its winner in the first two sentences, then spends the rest of the page explaining who should buy something else. Awards are written around a buyer — best for a trunk, best if you already own M12 — because a single "best" is useless when the constraint is your driveway, your car and the tools already in your garage.

Products are dropped when the manufacturer publishes no checkable figure at all. A brand that will not print a fill time, a bin volume or a durability claim has not given a buyer anything to hold it to, and each guide names those products in its "Also considered" section with the reason.
