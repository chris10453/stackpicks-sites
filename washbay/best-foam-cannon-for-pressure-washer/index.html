How to shop for a foam cannon

Published minimum GPM
Foam is made by forcing water and air through a small hole, and below the stated flow the cannon will not draw soap. Chemical Guys says 1.4 GPM minimum, 2.0 recommended; Adam's says 1.3; Greenworks rates its own at 1.1.
Orifice size, and whether you get a spare
The orifice sets back-pressure. MTM ships the PF22.2 with a 1.25 mm fitted and a 1.1 mm in the box so a low-flow electric washer can be made to foam; most cannons ship with one hole and no options.
Bottle capacity against one car
A sedan takes most of a quart of solution. Bottles here run 32 to 34 fl oz, which is one car and a bit — the difference between one fill and two on a truck.
Brass or plastic where it threads
The body takes the washer's full working pressure every time you pull the trigger. Brass bodies and metal plugs outlive plastic ones, and both MTM and Chemical Guys print which they use.
The quick-connect actually fitting your washer
Nearly everything here ships a standard 1/4-inch quick-connect plug. MTM notes that some residential machines need a low-profile plug instead, sold separately.
How much soap the maker tells you to use
Chemical Guys says 1 to 3 oz in the bottle; Adam's says 2 to 5 oz. That is a three-fold difference in running cost, and it is printed on the product pages rather than guessed at.

Questions people ask

What GPM do I need for a foam cannon?

At least the number the cannon's maker publishes. Chemical Guys asks for 1.4 GPM minimum and recommends 2.0; Adam's asks 1.3; Greenworks rates its own cannon at 1.1. A typical 2,000 PSI electric washer delivers 1.1 to 1.2 GPM, so match the cannon to the machine rather than the other way round.

How much soap goes in a foam cannon?

Chemical Guys says 1 to 3 oz of shampoo in the bottle, topped with warm water. Adam's says 2 to 5 oz in its 1 L bottle. Start at the bottom of the range — thicker foam is not the same as cleaner paint, and the mix that clings for two minutes is doing the work.

Does a foam cannon replace washing by hand?

No. Foam loosens and lubricates the top layer of grit so the mitt drags less of it across the paint. The two-bucket contact wash still does the cleaning; the foam step buys perhaps a third fewer passes on a dirty car.

What is the orifice and why does it matter?

It is the hole the water is forced through, and it sets the back-pressure the cannon builds. MTM ships the PF22.2 with a 1.25 mm fitted and a 1.1 mm spare; a smaller orifice raises pressure at the mixing chamber, which is how a lower-flow washer is made to foam.

Will a foam cannon fit my pressure washer?

If your wand takes a 1/4-inch quick-connect nozzle, almost certainly. MTM warns that some residential machines need a low-profile quick-connect plug, sold separately as part 24.5006. Check the nozzle end of your wand before ordering anything.

Also considered

  • Unbranded 1 L cannons sold only through marketplaces — No maker page, so no published minimum flow, no orifice size and no spare parts. When the foam is thin there is nothing to change and nobody to ask.
  • Foam guns that run off a garden hose — A different tool for a different household — worth buying if you have no pressure washer at all, which is why they get their own comparison rather than a place in this guide.
  • Cannons rated above 4,200 PSI for home use — The ceiling is irrelevant on an electric washer that tops out near 2,000 PSI, and the higher-pressure units tend to have stiffer knobs and coarser adjustment at the flow you will actually run.
