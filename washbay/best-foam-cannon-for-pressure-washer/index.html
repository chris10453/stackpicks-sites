An orifice size in millimeters is a hole diameter, not a flow rating: swapping MTM's 1.25 mm for the 1.1 mm lowers a related MTM kit's published minimum from 1.8 to 1.4 GPM, and it does not turn a 1.2 GPM washer into a match.

How to shop for a foam cannon

Published minimum GPM
Below the stated flow the cannon is not promised to draw soap. The minimums are in the fit table at the end of the page.
Orifice size, and whether you get a spare
The orifice sets back-pressure. MTM ships the PF22.2 with a 1.25 mm fitted and a 1.1 mm in the box; no other maker here lists a spare orifice. MTM's lowest published minimum with the smaller hole is still 1.4 GPM.
Bottle capacity against one car
Bottles here run 32 to 34 fl oz; Chemical Guys says its 34 oz Big Mouth covers an SUV or truck in one sweep.
Brass or plastic where it threads
The body takes working pressure on every pull. MTM and the TORQ print their metals, Greenworks lists plastic, and the Big Mouth page names none.
The quick-connect actually fitting your washer
Most ship a standard 1/4-inch quick-connect plug. MTM notes some residential machines need a low-profile plug, sold separately.
How much soap the maker tells you to use
Chemical Guys says 1 to 3 oz in the bottle; Adam's says 2 to 5 oz, a wide gap in running cost.

Questions people ask

What GPM do I need for a foam cannon?

At least the minimum its maker publishes, measured against your washer's rated flow. Chemical Guys asks for 1.4 GPM and recommends 2.0; Adam's asks 1.3; MTM advises 1.4 for the PF22.2; Greenworks prints no minimum, which means unknown, not approved. A washer rated 1.2 GPM is under all of the printed numbers.

How much soap goes in a foam cannon?

Chemical Guys says 1 to 3 oz of shampoo in the bottle, topped with warm water. Adam's says 2 to 5 oz in its 1 L bottle. Start at the bottom of the maker's range and add soap only if the foam runs off at once.

Does a foam cannon replace washing by hand?

No. Foam loosens and lubricates the top layer of grit so the mitt drags less of it across the paint. The two-bucket contact wash still does the cleaning.

What is the orifice, and is 1.1 mm a flow rating?

The orifice is the hole the water is forced through, and its size is a diameter in millimeters. It is not a flow rating. Flow (GPM) is how much water your pump delivers. MTM's manual for a related kit lists 1.8 GPM minimum with the 1.25 mm orifice and 1.4 GPM with the 1.1 mm. Swapping to the smaller one lowers that published minimum, and it does not guarantee foam on a washer below it.

Will a foam cannon fit my pressure washer?

If your wand takes a 1/4-inch quick-connect nozzle, almost certainly. MTM warns that some residential machines need a low-profile quick-connect plug, sold separately as part 24.5006. Check the nozzle end of your wand before ordering anything.

Also considered

  • Unbranded 1 L cannons sold only through marketplaces — No maker page, so no published minimum flow, no orifice size and no spare parts. When the foam is thin there is nothing to change.
  • Foam guns that run off a garden hose — A different tool for a different household, which is why they get their own comparison rather than a place in this guide.
  • Cannons rated above 4,200 PSI for home use — The ceiling is irrelevant on an electric washer that tops out near 2,000 PSI.

Will it work on my washer? GPM, orifice size and the limits

Three numbers get mixed up here. GPM is flow, gallons of water per minute. PSI is pressure, and every cannon accepts a wide band of it. The orifice is a hole inside the cannon, measured by its width in millimeters, so a 1.1 mm orifice is not a 1.1 GPM rating.

A smaller hole passes less water at the same pressure. MTM's paperwork shows the effect: its manual for the related 14.5014 foam kit asks for a minimum of 1.8 GPM and 1,100 PSI with the 1.25 mm orifice and 1.4 GPM and 1,100 PSI with the 1.1 mm. The hole's area falls to 0.77 of the original (1.1 squared over 1.25 squared) and the minimum to 0.78 (1.4 over 1.8). That match is our arithmetic, not an MTM statement. The PF22.2's own manual gives one figure, 1.4 GPM and 1,100 PSI "strongly advised", without saying which orifice it assumes.

Use your washer's rated flow, not its maximum. Westinghouse prints the ePX3100 as 1.76 max GPM "at lowest pressure" and 1.24 rated GPM at 1,900 rated PSI; the rated figure is the one to compare. Sun Joe prints the SPX3000 as 1.2 GPM certified rated flow at 2,030 PSI.
