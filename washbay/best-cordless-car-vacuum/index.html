A car interior is a hostile job: 3-inch gaps between seat rails, sand welded into carpet by a wet floor mat, and a power outlet that dies with the ignition — which is why a cordless handheld beats the shop vac in the garage. If you already own cordless tools, buy the vacuum that takes the battery you have — the DEWALT wet/dry on 20V MAX, the Milwaukee M18 2-gallon on M18, or the CRAFTSMAN V20 kit if you are starting from nothing.

Air flow, not suction, is the number that moves sand. Where a manufacturer publishes CFM it is in the spec block; where a brand publishes nothing but a marketing multiple, the table shows a dash rather than a borrowed figure.

How to shop for a cordless car vacuum

Air flow in CFM, if it is published
Sealed suction moves a coin; air flow moves sand out of carpet pile. Milwaukee and CRAFTSMAN publish 45 CFM, WORX publishes 22 CFM, and several brands publish nothing at all — which tells you something on its own.
Bin volume you can empty over a bag
A 7 oz dirt cup fills twice doing one footwell. Anything from 750 mL up does a whole car without a trip to the trash, and a 2-gallon tank does the car plus the garage floor.
A crevice tool that stays with the vacuum
The tool you need is the narrow one, and it is always the one missing. On-board storage is the difference between a two-minute job and a hunt through a drawer.
Wet pickup, or a clear warning that there is none
Spilled coffee and a wet floor mat are the most common interior emergencies. Two of these six are rated for wet debris; the WORX is explicitly dry-only and liquid will damage it.
Filters you can wash and re-buy
A filter sold as a proprietary cartridge at a quarter of the tool price is a subscription. DEWALT, WORX and CRAFTSMAN all publish washing instructions, which keeps the running cost near zero.
Battery platform before brand loyalty
A bare tool on a battery you already own is the cheapest capable vacuum you can buy. Buying a sixth charger for a sixth platform is how garages end up with a shelf of dead packs.

How we picked

  1. None of these vacuums has been run over a carpet by us. The ranking is built from what each manufacturer publishes — air flow in CFM, bin volume, filtration rating, run time — because sealed-suction multiples and kPa marketing figures are not comparable across brands. A dash in the table means the brand publishes no figure for that row.
  2. Every pick was measured on paper against three real gaps: the 2 to 3-inch channel a front seat rail sits in, the wedge between a seat base and the console, and the space under a rear bench. A vacuum needing a tool you have to go and find fails all three.
  3. Wet pickup is a hard yes or no, taken from the manufacturer's own rating rather than an assumption. Running a dry-only vacuum over a wet mat is how they die.
  4. Filters were checked for two things: whether the brand says you can wash them, and whether a replacement has its own part number. Milwaukee lists its HEPA filter as 49-90-1900; that is the standard the others were held to.
  5. Battery platform was treated as part of the price. A bare tool is only cheap when the batteries are already in the garage, and every bare-tool pick says so in its spec block.

Questions people ask

Is a cordless car vacuum strong enough, or do I need a shop vac?

For interiors, cordless wins on reach. The strongest cordless unit here publishes 45 CFM and 32 inches of water lift, which pulls sand out of carpet pile; the advantage a corded shop vac has is run time, not what it lifts out of a footwell you can reach.

How long does a car interior take to vacuum?

About 20 minutes for a sedan: 5 minutes per front footwell including the seat rails, 5 for the rear bench, 5 for the trunk and mats. A 7 oz dirt cup needs emptying two or three times in that job; a 750 mL bin does not need emptying at all.

Wet/dry or dry only?

Dry only is fine for most car messes and costs less. Buy wet/dry if you have children, a dog, or a leaking sunroof — the two wet-rated picks here are the DEWALT DCV517B and the Milwaukee 0880-20, and both also handle a flooded garage floor.

Also considered

  • 12-volt socket-powered car vacuums — They run off the accessory socket, so the engine has to run or the battery drains, and the cord reaches three quarters of the way around the car. Cordless solved this a decade ago.
  • Shark UltraCyclone Pet Pro+ — A capable handheld, but Shark publishes no air flow, no bin volume in millilitres and no filtration rating for it, so there is nothing to compare against the picks above.
  • Bissell handheld car vacuums — Same gap: suction claims and run-time estimates, but no published CFM or bin volume to hold them to.
  • Corded 2.5-gallon garage vacuums — Better value per gallon, wrong tool for an interior — the session goes on moving an extension cord, and the hose is too stiff for seat rails.
