How to shop for a wash mitt and bucket

A published pile weight or blend
Adam's prints 1,500 GSM and a 70/30 microfiber blend; The Rag Company prints 80/20. Most sellers in this aisle print adjectives instead, and an adjective cannot be checked against the mitt that arrives.
Size you can check before it arrives
The Rag Company publishes 8 × 10 in and Adam's publishes 9.75 × 8 in for the lambskin. A mitt two inches smaller than you expected means more passes per panel, which is more chances to drag something.
Something between the grit and the paint
A grit screen or a funnel insert sits in the bottom of the bucket and keeps the sand you just rinsed out below the water you are dipping into. Chemical Guys publishes close to 300 funnels on the Cyclone Dirt Trap; Adam's publishes that its screen fits 3.5 and 5 gallon round buckets.
Whether it survives the washing machine
Every mitt here is machine washable, which sounds like a given until you own the one that is not. The pile is what does the work, and a mitt that mats after four washes has become a sponge with a cuff.
Two buckets, not one
One bucket holds soap, the other holds rinse water. Adam's sells its 5 gallon bucket with the screen already in it, which is the cheapest way to make the two-bucket method a habit rather than a plan.

Questions people ask

Is a wash mitt really safer than a sponge?

Yes, and the mechanism is pile depth rather than softness. A flat sponge traps grit on its face and drags it along the clear coat. A 1,500 GSM mitt carries that grit up into the fibers, away from the paint, until you rinse it out — which is why the two-bucket rinse step is not optional with either tool.

What does GSM mean on a wash mitt?

Grams per square meter, the weight of fabric per unit area, and on a mitt it is a proxy for how deep the pile is. Adam's publishes 1,500 GSM for its microfiber mitt. Most makers publish nothing, which is why so few figures appear in the table above.

Do I need a grit guard if I already use two buckets?

Two buckets and no screen still works, but the rinse bucket becomes a suspension of sand that redistributes every time you dunk. A screen or a funnel insert gives the grit somewhere to settle below the water you are dipping into, for about the cost of a car wash.

How do I wash a wash mitt?

Cold machine wash with a microfiber detergent, no fabric softener and no dryer sheet — both coat the fibers and kill the pile that is doing the work. Air dry or tumble on the lowest setting. Every mitt in this guide is described by its maker as machine washable.

How many mitts should I own?

Two at minimum. The bottom third of a car carries the brake dust and road grit, and a mitt that has been down there does not belong back on the roof. That is why the two-pack in this guide takes the value spot.

Also considered

  • Unbranded chenille mitts sold only through marketplaces — No maker page, so no published blend, no pile weight and no size. When the pile mats after four washes there is nothing to compare against and nobody to ask.
  • Sponges and chamois cloths — A flat face holds grit against the paint rather than up inside a pile. They dry a car adequately and wash one badly, which is why this site's drying guide covers the cloths and this one does not.
  • Wash brushes on a pole — Useful on the roof of a van and hard to keep clean. The bristles pick up grit from the first panel and carry it to the last, and no maker in this category publishes a bristle specification worth reading.
  • Bucket lid seats without a screen — A seat is comfortable and does nothing for your paint. Buy the insert first and the lid second, in that order.
