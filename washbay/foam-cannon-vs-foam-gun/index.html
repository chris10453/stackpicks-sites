Foam cannon vs foam gun (2026) — which one to buy

This is a decision about water, not about foam. A cannon forces water through a small orifice at 800 PSI or more and whips air into it; a gun mixes soap into a garden hose stream at roughly 40 to 60 PSI. Both put soap on the car. Only one of them produces the shaving-cream layer people buy these things for, and it needs a machine that costs more than the cannon does.

The honest version: the foam layer is a pre-wash that loosens and lubricates grit so the mitt drags less of it. It does not replace the contact wash. A gun on a hose achieves most of that benefit for a fraction of the setup, which is why the answer depends entirely on what is already in your garage.

How to shop for a foam sprayer

What each one needs to work
A cannon needs a pressure washer meeting its published minimum — 1.4 GPM on the Chemical Guys units, 1.3 on the Adam's, 1.1 on the Greenworks. A gun needs a garden hose and nothing else.
What the foam is actually for
Lubrication and dwell. Thick foam clings while it softens road film so fewer particles ride under the mitt. Neither tool cleans the car on its own.
Soap used per wash
Chemical Guys asks 1 to 3 oz in the bottle for both the cannon and the gun; Adam's asks 2 to 5 oz in its cannon. A gun uses less soap per wash than most cannons because the mix is thinner.
Total cost to first foam
A gun is one purchase. A cannon is a cannon plus a pressure washer that fits it — the cheapest machine in our roundup lands well above the price of any cannon here.
Where you wash
An apartment parking space with a tap is a foam gun. A driveway with an outdoor outlet is a cannon, and the washer earns its keep on the patio and the bins as well.

Questions people ask

Does a foam cannon clean better than a foam gun?

It holds soap on the paint longer, which means more dwell time to soften road film. On a lightly soiled car after a contact wash, the finished result is the same — the difference shows on a car that has been sitting dirty for a month.

What pressure washer do I need for a foam cannon?

One that meets the cannon's published minimum flow: 1.4 GPM for the Chemical Guys cannons, 1.3 GPM for the Adam's, 1.1 GPM for the Greenworks. Pressure requirements start as low as 500 PSI, so flow is the number to check first.

How much soap goes in a foam gun?

Chemical Guys says 1 to 3 oz of car wash soap topped with fresh water in the Foam Blaster bottle — the same dose it gives for its pressure washer cannons.

Can I use dish soap in either?

No. Dish soap strips wax and sealant and dries out rubber trim. Use a pH-neutral car shampoo; the dilution ratios for the common ones are in our soap guide.

Is the foam step worth it at all?

It is worth about a third fewer mitt passes on a dirty car, which is the main source of swirl marks. It is not worth skipping the two-bucket wash, which is what actually cleans the paint.

Also considered

  • Pump-up garden sprayers as foam makers — They wet the car with soapy water, which is a pre-soak rather than foam. Fine, cheap, and not what either product above is being compared on.
  • Cordless battery foam sprayers — Convenient in a parking space with no tap, but the tanks are small and the foam is wetter than a hose gun's. A niche tool for a specific problem.
  • Hose-end soap dispensers that came with the washer — The onboard tank on a pressure washer applies soap at low pressure through a black nozzle. It works, it does not foam, and every cannon exists because of that.
