Water-based vs silicone tire shine: which one belongs on your car

By Chris Y. · Founder & editor Updated September 12, 2026

Almost every argument about tire shine is really an argument about sling — the black spatter up the side of a car that appears in the first mile after dressing the tires. Sling is a chemistry problem, and two of the biggest names in this category publish their chemistry, which makes the comparison unusually clean.

Chemical Guys states that Tire Kicker is water-based and dries to the touch. Adam's states that Adam's Tire Shine is silicone-based with added silica resins and calls it low-sling. Those two sentences predict everything that follows: the water-based bottle is safer and slightly flatter, the silicone bottle is darker and wetter and asks you to wait.

Buy the water-based one if you drive off straight after washing, park on a pale driveway, or have light paint. Buy the silicone one if what you want is the deep wet look and you can give it a few minutes.

How to shop for a tire dressing

What the maker says the base is
This is the one specification that predicts sling, and it is the one most brands leave off the bottle. Both products here publish it, which is why these two are the comparison rather than any other pair in the category.
How soon you can drive
Chemical Guys sells Tire Kicker as drying to the touch with no wipe-off step. Adam's says its formula dries to the touch in minutes and calls it low-sling, which is a weaker claim than no-sling and should be read as one.
How dark the finish goes
Silicone with silica resins produces a deeper, wetter black than a water-based dressing can. That is the entire reason the silicone product still exists, and it is a matter of taste rather than performance.
What else the bottle is rated for
Chemical Guys rates Tire Kicker for any rubber, vinyl and plastic that looks faded, so it covers trim and wheel arch liners. Adam's aims its dressing specifically at large tread blocks and raised sidewall lettering, which is a truck and off-road specialism.

Questions people ask

Does water-based tire shine last as long?

Usually not quite. Both of these are wash-to-wash products measured in one to two weeks. The long-lasting option in this category is neither — it is a graphene or acrylic spray coating with a dwell time and a wipe-off step, which this site's tire guide covers separately.

How do I stop tire shine slinging onto my paint?

Use less of it, wipe the excess with a foam applicator or a towel, and give it the few minutes each maker asks for before driving. Chemical Guys states Tire Kicker dries to the touch; Adam's calls its formula low-sling. Over-application beats either claim.

Is silicone bad for tires?

Dressings formulated for tires by these brands are made for the rubber they go on. The historic damage stories come from petroleum-based household substitutes, which strip the anti-ozonant compounds a tire uses to resist cracking.

Can I use either one on plastic trim?

Chemical Guys rates Tire Kicker for rubber, vinyl and plastic that appears faded or dull. Adam's aims its tire dressing at tires specifically. If trim is part of the job, that rating is the reason to buy the water-based bottle.

Which one is better on a truck?

Adam's, on its own reasoning: it says the formula was tuned for detailed tread, raised lettering and large tread blocks, which is what light truck and all-terrain tires have. Park it up for half an hour afterwards.
