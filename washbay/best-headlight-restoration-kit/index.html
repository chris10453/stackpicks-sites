Yellow headlights are not dirt. The factory applies a hard UV clear coat to the polycarbonate lens, sun destroys that coat from the outside in, and what you are looking at is bare, oxidized plastic. That is what decides which kits work: anything that only polishes is removing the damaged layer and leaving raw plastic in the same sun that wrecked it. It looks perfect for about four months.

Badly clouded lenses cut faster with the Mothers NuLens kit and a drill; lightly hazy ones clear in ten minutes with the Turtle Wax Renew & Restore wipes, which leave no protection at all. One check first: wipe a lens with isopropyl alcohol. If it goes clear while wet, it is external oxidation and every kit here will work; if the fog is inside the housing, it is a failed seal and no kit will touch it.

How to shop for a headlight restoration kit

Does it put a protective coat back on?
This is the entire question. Sanding and polishing restores clarity; only a ceramic or sealant step stops the sun from re-oxidizing bare polycarbonate within a season. Three of these five have a protection step, and the two that do not say so up front.
Sanding discs, or abrasive wipes?
Heavy oxidation needs graded abrasives — CERAKOTE includes four sanding discs in two grits, Turtle Wax includes a sanding pad. Light haze does not need sanding at all, and sanding a lens that did not need it costs clear coat you cannot put back.
Tools you actually own
The NuLens kit needs a drill; CERAKOTE and both Turtle Wax kits need nothing but hands. A kit that needs a drill you do not have is not cheaper than one that does not.
Enough product for both headlights
CERAKOTE states each kit restores and protects two headlights. Kits that quietly cover one lens are the most common complaint in this category.
A published durability claim
Turtle Wax states its Lens-Coat ceramic wipes can last up to a year; CERAKOTE publishes a limited lifetime warranty. Kits with no published claim are, in practice, the ones that go yellow again first.
Refills sold separately
Mothers sells replacement sanding discs for the NuLens ball as a separate part. That turns a kit into a tool you keep rather than one you throw away after one pair of lenses.

Questions people ask

How long does headlight restoration take?

CERAKOTE publishes 30 minutes for both headlights across its three steps. A drill kit cuts that to roughly 15 minutes of working time on moderate oxidation; a wipe-only product is about 10 minutes and does nothing to protect the lens afterwards.

How long will the result last?

It depends entirely on the last step. CERAKOTE backs its ceramic coating with a limited lifetime warranty, Turtle Wax states up to a year for its Lens-Coat wipes, and a polish-only restoration typically starts hazing again within a season of full sun because the lens is bare polycarbonate.

Do I have to sand my headlights?

Only if the oxidation is heavy. Wipe the lens with isopropyl alcohol first: if it clears while wet, abrasive wipes are enough. If it stays cloudy you need graded sanding — CERAKOTE includes four discs in two grits, and the Mothers kit sands with a drill.

Will restoration fix fogging inside the housing?

No. Internal condensation is a failed seal or a missing vent plug, and nothing applied to the outside of the lens will clear it. That is a housing repair or a replacement.
