Interior brushes look interchangeable in a photograph and are not. A soft synthetic brush that can be used dry on a touchscreen is a different tool from a stiff nylon scrubber for floor mats, and using the second on the first's job is how piano-black trim gets hazed.

Everything below is judged on what the maker prints: bristle length and width in inches, bristle material, handle material, and whether the brush is rated for chemicals. Brushes with no published dimensions are named as such in the table rather than given a borrowed figure.

How to shop for an interior detailing brush

Published bristle length and width
Adam's prints 9 in by 1.75 in for the large brush, 6.25 in by 1 in for the small, 6.5 × 2.5 in for the Cockpit Brush and 5 in wide with 2.5 in bristles for the multi-purpose. Chemical Guys publishes none of these, which is the single biggest difference between the two ranges.
Bristle material against the surface
Soft synthetic and boar-type bristles are for screens, gauges and gloss trim. Nylon scrubbing bristles are for carpet, mats and rubber. One brush cannot do both jobs well, and the ones sold as if they can are usually too stiff for the delicate half.
Chemical resistance
Interior cleaners are alkaline. Adam's states its synthetic bristles are more resistant to strong chemicals than boar's hair, and describes the Cockpit Brush as chemical resistant. A brush that sheds after six cleanings is a brush you buy twice.
Metal-free construction
A ferrule with exposed metal will eventually meet a door card or a screen surround. Adam's publishes a metal-free design on its interior brushes; that is a small thing that matters at exactly the moment it fails.
Whether the handle survives a wet hand
Adam's uses a textured grip on the plastic brushes, hardwood on the Cockpit Brush and a molded rubberized grip on the large multi-purpose. Chemical Guys describes ergonomic grips without publishing what they are made of.
One brush or a set
A set solves the fact that vents, seams and cupholders all want different head sizes. It also means you own three brushes of unpublished size instead of one you can measure, which is the trade this page is really about.

Questions people ask

How many interior brushes do I actually need?

Two. One soft brush for screens, vents, trim and leather, and one stiff brush for carpet and rubber mats. The soft one cannot agitate a mat and the stiff one will haze gloss trim, and no single brush in this guide is sold as doing both.

Can I use a detailing brush dry?

Yes, and it is the fastest way to clear dust from an instrument cluster or a touchscreen. Adam's rates both of its interior brushes for dry use on gauges, nav screens and piano black trim. Wet the brush only when you are using a cleaner.

Are boar's hair brushes better than synthetic?

Not for interiors. Adam's states that its synthetic bristles are softer than boar's hair and more resistant to strong cleaning chemicals, and interior cleaners are exactly where that resistance is tested. Natural bristle still has a place on exterior badges and grilles.

Will a detailing brush scratch a touchscreen?

A soft synthetic brush used dry will not. The risk is the grit already sitting on the screen, so blow or lift the loose dust first and brush with almost no pressure. Metal-free construction, which Adam's publishes on its interior brushes, removes the other risk.

How do I clean a detailing brush?

Rinse in warm water with a drop of soap, work the bristles between your fingers until the water runs clear, shake out and dry bristles-down so the water leaves the ferrule. A brush left wet head-up loosens its own glue over a season.

Also considered

  • Drill-mounted brush attachments — Excellent on carpet and dangerous everywhere else. At drill speeds the difference between agitating a mat and burning a hole in it is about two seconds, and none of the sets sold for cars publish a safe speed.
  • Boar's hair brushes sold for interiors — Natural bristle holds up badly to alkaline interior cleaners and sheds into vents. Adam's makes the point itself when it explains why its interior bristles are synthetic.
  • Toothbrushes and household scrub brushes — The bristles are packed for a different job and the ferrules are metal. They work once, then they leave a mark on the trim surround you were cleaning around.
  • Interior brush sets with no published material — Several marketplace sets describe bristles only as soft. Without a material or a chemical-resistance claim there is no way to know whether an interior cleaner will dissolve them, and that failure shows up as bristles in your vents.
