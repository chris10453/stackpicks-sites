How to shop for a small French press

Total capacity, and the brew floor underneath it
A press works by holding the grounds under water and then under the screen. Too little liquid and the screen never reaches the bed. Espro publishes a minimum brew size — 24 oz on the 32 oz P3 — and nobody else on this page publishes one at all, which is why buying small is safer than buying big and under-filling.
Published outside dimensions
Both Hario presses print width, depth and height in millimeters. GROSCHE publishes a capacity and no dimensions. Espro publishes dimensions for some models and, on the P3, prints the same block for both sizes it sells. Cupboard space is a real constraint on a thing that lives on a counter.
One filter or two
A single mesh screen lets fines through, which is the silt in the last inch of a press cup. GROSCHE puts a second filter in the lid; Espro puts a second micro-filter inside the first and calls it double filtration. Both Harios are single-screen and taste like it, in the traditional way.
Glass or steel
Glass shows the brew and takes no flavor; steel keeps the coffee hot for the fifteen minutes a two-cup brew actually takes to drink, and survives a tiled floor. The two cheapest picks here are glass and the most expensive is double-walled steel.
What happens when the beaker breaks
It will. GROSCHE and Espro both sell replacement beakers as catalog items; Hario's presses come apart entirely for cleaning and the glass is a standard Hario part. Check that before you buy anything with a glass body.

Questions people ask

How much coffee do I need for a two-cup press?

Around 1 part coffee to 15 parts water by weight — so about 20 grams of coarse grounds for the Hario's 300 milliliters, or 23 grams for the GROSCHE's 350. Start there and adjust by taste rather than by the scoop that came in the box.

Why does my French press coffee have silt in the bottom?

A single mesh screen has holes big enough to let fines through, and a grinder that produces a lot of fines makes it worse. The two Espro presses filter twice and remove almost all of it; with a single-screen press, grind coarser and stop pouring an inch from the bottom. Our burr vs blade page covers where fines come from.

Can I brew one cup in a big French press?

Not well, and Espro is the brand that says so out loud: it prints a 24 oz minimum brew size on its 32 oz press. Below that the plunger screen stops above the coffee bed, so the grounds never get pressed and the coffee keeps extracting in the cup.

How long should a small press steep?

Four minutes from the pour, then plunge slowly and decant. A two-cup press cools much faster than an eight-cup one, which is the argument for the double-walled steel P7 — in glass, the coffee is lukewarm by the time you go back for the second cup.

What grind size does a French press need?

Coarse — the coarsest setting you would use for anything. Fine grounds slip through the screen, over-extract during the four-minute steep and make the plunge hard work. If your grinder cannot hold a coarse setting, that is the thing to fix first.
