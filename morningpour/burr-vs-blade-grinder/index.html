A blade is a propeller in a cup: it chops whatever is in the way until you let go of the button. A burr set passes beans between two surfaces a fixed distance apart, so nothing leaves until it is small enough to fall through the gap. The difference shows up in the cup as one identifiable flaw — bitter and sour at the same time, because the dust over-extracts while the boulders under-extract, and you drink both in the same mouthful.

How to shop for a coffee grinder

How the machine makes small pieces
A burr set cuts to a gap you choose. A blade impacts whatever it hits at random. One is a dimension you set; the other is a duration you guess.
Whether the result is repeatable
A burr grinder at setting 20 gives you the same grind tomorrow. A blade grinder gives you whatever eight seconds of chopping produced this time, which is never quite the same thing twice.
What the manufacturer will admit
Hamilton Beach's own support page concedes that all blade grinders produce some uneven grounds, and recommends a burr grinder for pour-over. That is the maker of the blade grinder in this comparison saying it.
Heat
A blade spins fast enough to warm the grounds while it works, and heat drives off aromatics before the water touches them. Burr sets run slower — Baratza publishes 550 RPM for the Encore.

The verdict

Burr, always, for coffee. A burr grinder sets a particle size; a blade grinder times a collision. That is a difference in kind rather than degree, and it produces the specific fault — bitter and sour in the same cup — that no brewer, ratio or brew time can correct afterwards.

Questions people ask

Is a burr grinder really worth it for drip coffee?

Yes, and drip is where it shows up most clearly, because a drip machine gives you no way to compensate. The cheapest fix is a hand grinder like the Timemore C3 ESP, which costs about what a blade grinder does.

Can I get a decent grind from a blade grinder if I shake it?

It helps, and Hamilton Beach's own instructions tell you to do it, because beans settle against the chamber wall and miss the blade. It reduces the problem; it does not fix it. You are still timing a chop rather than setting a size.

What is the cheapest burr grinder worth buying?

A hand grinder. The Timemore Chestnut C3 ESP has a 38 mm CNC-machined conical burr set adjusting in 0.0233 mm clicks, for less than most electric grinders of either type. The tradeoff is 20 grams per grind and roughly 30 to 60 seconds of cranking.

Is there anything a blade grinder is actually good at?

Whole spices. Uneven particle size is not a problem when you are grinding peppercorns or cumin into a dish, and a small dishwasher-safe chamber is genuinely convenient for it. Keep one in the drawer for that and grind coffee with burrs.

What the difference actually costs you, in one cup

Extraction is a race against surface area. Dust has enormous surface area for its mass, so hot water strips it fast and keeps going past the point where the good flavors stop and the bitter ones start. A boulder has very little, so the water barely gets into it and takes out only the sharp sour acids near the surface. Brew them together and the cup is two opposite mistakes at once.

That is why the usual fixes do not work. Grinding longer makes more dust without rescuing the boulders. Using more coffee scales the problem up. Brewing cooler softens the bitterness and worsens the sourness; brewing hotter does the reverse. No brewer setting separates two populations of particle after the fact — which is why Uman and colleagues published particle-size distributions measured by laser diffraction in Scientific Reports: distribution, not nominal grind size, predicts what comes out of the cup.

If you already own a blade grinder

Three things help, in descending order of effect, and none of them fix it.

  1. Shake the machine while it runs. Hamilton Beach's own support guidance for the Fresh Grind says to tilt or shake it so beans settling against the chamber wall are moved back into the blade path. It measurably evens things out and it is free.
  2. Pulse rather than hold. Short bursts with a shake between them spread the damage around instead of pulverising whatever sits directly under the blade.
  3. Grind coarser than feels right and brew by immersion. French press and cold brew tolerate an uneven grind far better than pour-over, because long steeping lets the boulders catch up. A blade grinder and a French press is an honest combination; a blade grinder and a V60 is not.

What does not help: buying better beans. If your budget is fixed, cheaper beans through a burr grinder beat good beans through a blade, every time — and the price assumption that keeps this argument alive is no longer true. A hand grinder with a CNC-machined 38 mm conical burr set now costs roughly what a name-brand blade grinder costs.
