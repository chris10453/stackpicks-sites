How to shop for a small-kitchen coffee maker

Height, and the clearance you actually have
KraftMaid publishes the standard installation — wall cabinets 54 inches above the floor over a 36-inch counter — so a normal kitchen leaves 18 inches. Most drip machines are 14 to 16 inches tall, which sounds fine until a rear-hinged lid has to open. No brand publishes the lid-open height, so the workaround is a machine that fills from the front or has a tank you lift out.
Footprint, depth over width
Wall cabinets are 12 inches deep and base cabinets are 24, so a machine that is shallow front-to-back buys more usable counter than one that is merely narrow. Depth is the number nobody compares and the one you run out of.
A published brew temperature
SCA Standard 310-2021 specifies 90 to 96 °C for a certified home brewer. Exactly two machines here carry a certification; the rest say "the right temperature" and leave it there. Where a brand publishes no wattage or no labeled height, that absence goes in the table as published.
Carafe type and whether there is a timer
A thermal carafe removes the hot plate, and a hot plate is what scorches a pot after about half an hour. Glass shows you the level and costs less. A 24-hour timer is the difference between coffee at 6 a.m. and coffee at 6:12, and two machines below are widely assumed to be programmable and are not — hence the Timer row.

Questions people ask

What is the tallest coffee maker that fits under a kitchen cabinet?

About 13 inches is the safe ceiling in a standard kitchen with 18 inches of clearance, because a rear-hinged reservoir lid has to open and no manufacturer publishes how much air that needs. The way around it is a machine that does not hinge upward at all: the Hamilton Beach FrontFill at 10.3 inches fills from the front, and the Zojirushi ZUTTO and Cuisinart 5-Cup have tanks you lift out at the sink.

Does a small coffee maker brew worse coffee?

Not necessarily, but small and cheap usually travel together, and cheap is what costs you temperature. The two machines here carrying an SCA certification are the Moccamaster and the OXO, and the three sub-11-inch machines publish no brew temperature at all.

Is a thermal carafe worth it in a small kitchen?

Yes, for a reason beyond heat: it removes the hot plate, which is both the part that scorches coffee after about 30 minutes and the part that stays hot next to everything else crowded onto the counter. The OXO 8-Cup has a double-wall vacuum carafe; the Cuisinart 5-Cup is a middle ground, stainless on a 30-minute warmer.

Can I run a coffee maker and a kettle on the same kitchen outlet?

Often not. A 20-ampere circuit at 120 volts carries 2,400 watts; the Moccamaster draws 1,475 and a 1,200-watt kettle on top of that is 2,675. The Zojirushi's 650 watts is the gentlest draw here. US kitchens must have at least two 20-ampere small-appliance circuits, so the fix is usually a different outlet.
