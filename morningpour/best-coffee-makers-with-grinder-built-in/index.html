How to shop for a coffee maker with a grinder built in

Height, measured against the cabinet
A grind-and-brew is a drip machine with a second appliance stacked on it. The hopper lid has to open, so the clearance you need is the published height plus a few inches of swing. Every height below comes from the manufacturer's own specification block, and the spread is 5.3 inches from the shortest to the tallest.
Burr or blade, and whether the brand says
Burrs shave beans to a repeatable size; blades smash them into a mix of dust and boulders, and the dust over-extracts while the boulders do not extract at all. Breville prints burr and Cuisinart prints blade. BLACK+DECKER and GEVI say nothing at all, which tells you something on its own.
Carafe type
Glass sits on a hotplate that keeps cooking the coffee; a dual-wall thermal carafe holds temperature with no heat applied. One machine here ships thermal, three ship glass. The thermal one is also the shortest, which is not a coincidence — no hotplate, no base.
Depth, because the hopper sits under the cabinet
Width is usually fine. Depth is what decides whether the machine can sit back far enough to clear an overhanging cabinet, and it runs from 8.13 to 10.7 inches here. Measure from the wall to the front edge of your counter before anything else.
What it costs to keep running
A grinder adds a second thing to clean and a second thing to break. Warranties here run from one year to three, and all four ship a permanent metal filter, so there is no paper to buy.

Questions people ask

Is a built-in grinder actually better than grinding separately?

A separate burr grinder is better coffee and a worse morning. One box means one thing to clean and one power outlet; two boxes mean you can buy a grinder that costs more than the whole machine here. If you already own a burr grinder, a plain drip maker is the better spend — our small-kitchen drip guide covers those.

How much clearance do I need above a grind-and-brew?

The published height plus enough room to swing the hopper lid — allow three inches over the number in the table. That makes the Breville a 15.5-inch job and the GEVI a 20.8-inch one, against a standard counter-to-cabinet gap of 18 inches.

Do these machines grind into the basket or into a separate chamber?

All four grind straight into the brew basket. None of them publishes what happens to the grounds that stick to the chute on the way down, which is the part that needs cleaning most often.

Can I use pre-ground coffee in one?

Yes, on all four. Every machine here has a grinder-off setting. It is the feature to check on any grind-and-brew, because it is what keeps the machine useful the week the mill jams.

Which of these has a thermal carafe?

Only the Breville, which ships a 12-cup dual-wall stainless carafe. The other three are glass on a hotplate. That is also why the Breville is the shortest machine here — there is no heated base under the pot.
