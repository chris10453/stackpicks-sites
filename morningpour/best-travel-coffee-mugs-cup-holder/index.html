How to shop for a travel coffee mug

A published body width
This is the only number that decides the question in the title, and four of the five products here publish it. A cup holder is a hole; a mug is a cylinder; the mug fits or it does not. Go and measure the holder in your own car before you read any further — it takes ten seconds and it settles the argument.
Height, and where the lid opens
Height decides whether the mug clears the center console lid and whether it will stand upright in a bag. The spread here is 7.09 to 10.2 inches, and the tallest is the one that looks most like a water bottle.
What the lid does with one hand
A commuting mug is operated at a red light. Stanley's trigger lid and Fellow's flip lid both work with a thumb; a screw lid does not, and a straw lid is a cold-drink answer to a hot-drink question.
The published heat rating
Every product here except one prints an hours-hot and hours-cold figure. They are marketing numbers measured under conditions nobody publishes, so treat them as a ranking rather than a promise — but a brand that prints 12 hours and one that prints 6 are telling you something real about the wall thickness.
What the inside is made of
Bare stainless picks up a metallic edge in coffee that some people taste and some do not. Fellow lines both of its mugs with ceramic and says so; Stanley and Thermos use plain 18/8 steel.

Questions people ask

How wide is a car cup holder?

It depends on the car, which is why this page ranks on published width rather than quoting one figure. Put a tape measure across the top of your own holder and write the number down; then compare it against 3.03 inches for both Fellow mugs, 3.06 for the Stanley Café-To-Go and 3.29 for the Stanley Everyday Suburban.

Do any of these fit under a coffee machine?

The two short ones are the candidates — 6.2 inches for the Carter Move and 7.09 for the Everyday Suburban. Breville's Grind Control brews directly into a travel mug, and our grind-and-brew guide covers the machines that offer that.

Are the hours-hot ratings real?

Treat them as a ranking, not a promise. None of these brands publishes the starting temperature, the ambient temperature or the cut-off they measured against, so 12 hours from Fellow and 7 from Thermos tells you the Fellow is better insulated and nothing more precise than that.

Does a ceramic lining actually change the taste?

It removes the metallic edge some people get from bare stainless, especially with light roasts and with coffee that sits for an hour. Both Fellow mugs are ceramic-lined; the Stanley and Thermos mugs are plain 18/8 steel inside.

What size should I buy?

Match it to your cup, not to the biggest number. A 12 oz mug holds a medium coffee-shop drink; 16 oz holds a large; the Carter Carry is a whole-day bottle at 10.2 inches tall. Buying bigger than you drink means carrying cold coffee at lunchtime.
