A Bambino-class machine — the Bambino Plus, the Dedica, a Gaggia Classic — is a 54 or 58 mm portafilter with a real pump behind it and no grinder inside. Fill Breville's published 18-gram basket with what a general-purpose grinder produces and the shot runs in eight seconds or chokes, and no tamping fixes it: the grinder cannot make a small enough change.

That is the selection criterion here, and it is a number: how finely you can adjust inside the espresso range. Everything below publishes it in some form — microns per click, clicks per rotation, steps inside the band, or stepless — and this guide ranks on it.

How to shop for an espresso grinder

How small a change you can make near espresso
Espresso lives in a grind range a few tens of microns wide, so 40 settings spread across every brew method leave perhaps four usable espresso positions. 1Zpresso publishes 8 microns per click, Timemore 0.0233 mm, Baratza 20 of 40 steps inside the espresso range, and the DF54 is stepless.
Burr type and size
Flat burrs cut a narrower particle distribution and tend to give a sweeter, more separated shot; conicals are more forgiving and cheaper to build. Size sets the throughput: 54 mm flat (DF54), 48 mm conical (J-Ultra), 40 mm conical (Encore ESP), 38 mm conical (C3 ESP). Bigger is faster, not automatically better.
Retention, and whether the dose you weighed is the dose you get
Grounds left inside a grinder come out stale in the next shot, which is why single-dosing exists. MiiCoffee publishes under 0.1 gram of retention on the DF54 and nobody else here publishes the figure at all — which tells you which were designed for espresso and which were adapted to it.
Footprint, because the machine is already on the counter
A Bambino Plus is 11.9 by 7.4 inches before you add anything. The DF54 is 4.5 inches wide and 12 tall, the Dose Control Pro 8 by 6.3 by 13.5, the Encore ESP 13 by 15 centimetres, and the hand grinders take a drawer.
What the manufacturer will actually publish
Two of the five brands here publish burr size, adjustment resolution, capacity, dimensions and weight. Breville publishes a settings count and a dimension string and no burr size. Where a brand leaves a figure out, this guide names the brand and says so.

Questions people ask

Can the plain Baratza Encore grind for espresso?

Not usefully. Both use the same 40 mm conical burrs in different ranges: the ESP puts 20 of its 40 macro steps inside the espresso band, where the plain Encore bottoms out at a fine drip grind. That is the whole difference, and it is the difference between a shot and a gusher.

Do I need a single-dose grinder, or is a hopper fine?

A hopper is fine if you drink one kind of coffee. Single-dosing exists because retained grounds come out stale in the next shot — MiiCoffee publishes under 0.1 gram on the DF54 and no one else here publishes the number. If you switch beans often, retention decides it.

How fine is espresso grind, in numbers?

Fine enough that useful adjustments are measured in tens of microns. 1Zpresso publishes 8 microns per click for the J-Ultra and Timemore 23 microns for the C3 ESP, and one click on either visibly moves a shot's run time. That is why settings spread evenly across every brew method fail at espresso.

Is a hand grinder realistic for daily espresso?

For one or two shots, yes — thirty to forty seconds of cranking for an 18-gram dose, more on the 38 mm C3 ESP than the 48 mm J-Ultra. For four shots on a weekend morning, no, which is why the electric picks rank where they do.

Will a better grinder fix a pressurised basket?

No, and this is worth knowing before you spend. A pressurised dual-wall basket creates its own back-pressure and largely ignores your grind, which is why it works with pre-ground. The grinder starts to matter when you move to the single-wall basket — and then it matters more than anything else on the counter.
