A pour-over scale has two jobs. It weighs coffee and water to a tenth of a gram — the SCA ratio is 55 grams per kilogram, and a 20-gram dose that is really 22 is a 10% error. And it runs a timer in the same glance, because how long the water takes is the other half of what you control.

Everything past those two jobs is comfort, and comfort is where these five separate. The ranking is built on the figures each manufacturer publishes, and where a brand publishes neither a capacity nor a dimension the table says so by name.

How to shop for a coffee scale

Readability, and the minimum weight it will notice
Every scale here reads to 0.1 g, so readability alone separates nothing. Minimum detection does: Timemore publishes 0.5 g for the Basic 2.0, the smallest amount it will register at all. A scale that ignores the first gram of water starts your brew late.
Maximum capacity, against a full brewer
A Chemex weighs about 600 grams empty and takes 30 ounces of water, so a 2,000-gram scale is comfortable and a 1,000-gram one is not. Fellow publishes 2,500 g, Timemore 2 kg, Hario names 2000 in the model designation. Greater Goods publishes no maximum at all.
The timer, and whether it starts itself
A separate stopwatch is one more thing to press with a full kettle in your hand. Every scale here has an integrated timer; what differs is whether it counts down as well as up (Greater Goods and Fellow do) and whether it starts itself when water hits the pan — Timemore's wake-up timer on the Basic Mini.
What happens when you spill on it
You will pour hot water onto this object. Timemore publishes a waterproof dial surface and silicone pad for the Basic 2.0 and IPX3 for the Mini; Greater Goods ships a removable dishwasher-safe cover; Fellow says plainly that the Tally Pro is water resistant and not waterproof — worth more than a marketing claim.
Power, and how often you have to think about it
The runtimes are published: Timemore 1600 mAh and up to 10 hours on the Basic 2.0, 760 mAh on the Mini, Fellow 13 hours continuous from a one-to-two-hour USB-C charge. Continuous hours matter less than they sound — a brew is four minutes — but a scale that auto-sleeps after three, as the Basic 2.0 does, lasts weeks per charge.

Questions people ask

Do I really need a scale for pour-over coffee?

Yes, more than you need a better dripper. The SCA ratio is 55 grams of coffee per kilogram of water — about 19 grams for a 350-gram brew — and a scoop is routinely out by two or three grams, a 10 to 15% error before the water moves.

What resolution does a coffee scale need?

0.1 gram, which every scale here provides. What separates them is the minimum weight the scale registers at all: Timemore publishes 0.5 g for the Basic 2.0 and no one else here publishes the figure at all.

Can I use a kitchen scale instead?

A 1-gram kitchen scale is not enough resolution for a 19-gram dose, and most have no timer and a slow refresh. A 0.1-gram jewellery-type scale weighs coffee fine; you will just run a separate stopwatch.

How big should the scale be for a Chemex?

Capacity first: a Chemex Six Cup is about 600 grams empty and takes 30 ounces, so aim for 2,000 grams or more — Fellow publishes 2,500 g, Timemore 2 kg. Shape second: the Hario at 120 by 190 mm is deep enough that brewer and server sit fully on it.

Are waterproof coffee scales worth paying for?

Worth reading rather than paying extra for. Timemore publishes IPX3 on the Mini and a waterproof dial plus silicone pad on the Basic 2.0; Fellow states the Tally Pro is water resistant and not waterproof; Greater Goods ships a removable dishwasher-safe cover — arguably the best answer, on the least costly scale.
