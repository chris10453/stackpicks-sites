A pour-over scale has exactly two jobs. It has to weigh the coffee and the water to a tenth of a gram, because the SCA's standard brew ratio is 55 grams of coffee per kilogram of water and a 20-gram dose that is really 22 is a 10% error. And it has to run a timer in the same glance, because how long the water takes to go through is the other half of what you are controlling.

Everything past those two jobs is comfort, and comfort is where these five separate: a backlit screen, a flow-rate readout, a silicone cover that survives a spill, a battery that lasts a fortnight. The ranking below is built on the figures each manufacturer publishes, and where a brand will not publish a maximum capacity or a dimension — Greater Goods publishes neither — the table says so.

How to shop for a coffee scale

Readability, and the minimum weight it will notice
Every scale here reads to 0.1 g, so readability alone separates nothing. The figure that does is minimum detection: Timemore publishes 0.5 g for the Basic 2.0, which is the smallest amount the scale will register at all. A scale that ignores the first gram of water is a scale that starts your brew late.
Maximum capacity, against a full brewer
A Chemex weighs about 600 grams empty and takes 30 ounces of water, so a 2,000-gram scale is comfortable and a 1,000-gram one is not. Fellow publishes 2,500 g for the Tally Pro, Timemore publishes 2 kg for the Basic 2.0, Hario names 2000 in the model designation. Greater Goods publishes no maximum at all.
The timer, and whether it starts itself
A separate stopwatch is one more thing to press with a full kettle in your hand. Every scale here has an integrated timer; the differences are whether it counts down as well as up (Greater Goods and Fellow both do) and whether it starts on its own when water hits the pan, which Timemore calls a wake-up timer on the Basic Mini.
What happens when you spill on it
You will pour hot water onto this object. Timemore publishes a waterproof dial surface and a silicone insulation pad for the Basic 2.0 and an IPX3 rating for the Mini; Greater Goods ships a removable, dishwasher-safe silicone cover; Fellow says plainly that the Tally Pro is water resistant but not waterproof. That last sentence is worth more than a marketing claim.
Power, and how often you have to think about it
Rechargeable is now standard and the runtimes are published: Timemore 1600 mAh and up to 10 hours on the Basic 2.0, 760 mAh on the Mini, Fellow 13 hours of continuous use from a USB-C charge that takes one to two hours. Continuous hours matter less than they sound — a brew is four minutes — but a scale that auto-sleeps after three minutes, as the Basic 2.0 does, lasts weeks per charge.

Questions people ask

Do I really need a scale for pour-over coffee?

Yes, more than you need a better dripper. The SCA's standard ratio is 55 grams of coffee per kilogram of water — about 19 grams for a 350-gram brew — and a scoop is out by two or three grams routinely, which is a 10 to 15% error in strength before the water ever moves. A scale is the cheapest repeatability you can buy.

What resolution does a coffee scale need?

0.1 gram, which every scale here provides. What separates them is the minimum weight the scale will register at all: Timemore publishes 0.5 g for the Basic 2.0 and nobody else on this page publishes the figure. A scale that ignores the first gram of water starts your timer late and reads your total low.

Can I use a kitchen scale instead?

A 1-gram kitchen scale is not enough resolution for a 19-gram dose, and most have no timer and a slow refresh, so the number lags your pour by a second or two. If you already own a 0.1-gram jewellery-type scale it will weigh coffee fine; you will just be running a separate stopwatch.

How big should the scale be for a Chemex?

Capacity first: a Chemex Six Cup is about 600 grams empty and takes 30 ounces of water, so aim for 2,000 grams or more — the Fellow Tally Pro publishes 2,500 g and the Timemore Basic 2.0 publishes 2 kg. Shape second: the Hario at 120 by 190 mm is deliberately deep so a brewer and server sit fully on it.

Are waterproof coffee scales worth paying for?

The ratings are worth reading rather than paying extra for. Timemore publishes IPX3 on the Basic Mini and a waterproof dial surface plus a silicone pad on the Basic 2.0; Fellow states the Tally Pro is water resistant and not waterproof; Greater Goods ships a removable dishwasher-safe cover. The removable cover is arguably the best of the three answers and it is on the cheapest scale.
