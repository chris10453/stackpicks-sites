Cold brew is the one coffee where the brewer lives in the refrigerator for twelve to twenty-four hours, so the appliance competes with your food for space. That is a real problem in an apartment fridge and almost no review mentions it. Three of the five makers below publish a capacity and no dimensions at all.

So this guide is ranked on published size first and coffee second, the way our small-kitchen drip guide is ranked on cabinet clearance. Measure your fridge first.

How to shop for a cold brew coffee maker

Published dimensions, not just capacity
A quart is a quart no matter what shape it comes in, and shape is what decides whether it stands in a door bin, lies on a shelf or has to displace the milk. Two brands here publish width, depth and height — Hario for its pitcher, and Toddy for both its brewing container and its decanter. Takeya publishes a capacity and a weight. asobu publishes a capacity that does not match its own Amazon page.
Whether it can lie down
An airtight, leakproof lid turns a tall brewer into a short one, because it can lie on its side on a shelf. Both Takeyas publish one; the Hario and the Toddy do not, so their height is their height.
Ready-to-drink or concentrate
A mesh-filter pitcher makes coffee you pour and drink. The Toddy makes a concentrate you dilute, so one brew of the biggest object here yields far more finished coffee than one brew of the smallest. The fridge maths is not the capacity maths.
What the filter is, and what it costs to keep using
A permanent stainless mesh costs nothing after purchase and lets some fines through. The Toddy's felt filter plus optional paper bags is a cleaner cup and a running cost — the same trade as paper versus metal in a pour-over.
Glass or plastic
Glass holds no odour and no stain; Tritan does not shatter when a wet hand meets a tile floor. Takeya publishes both versions of the same quart brewer, 526 g in glass and 467 g in Tritan — the cleanest either-or on the page.

Questions people ask

How long does cold brew take, and does longer mean stronger?

The published steeps here run from 8 hours (asobu) to 24 (both Takeyas and the Toddy). Longer extracts more, but past about 24 hours you are adding bitterness rather than strength — 12 to 18 hours at room temperature, or the full 24 in the fridge, then take the grounds out.

How much coffee do I use for cold brew?

Around 1 part coffee to 8 parts water by weight — roughly 75 grams of coarse grounds for the Hario's 600 mL. Concentrate is far stronger: Toddy takes 12 ounces of coffee to 7 cups of water, and you dilute the result.

Does cold brew need to be made in the refrigerator?

No — room temperature works and extracts faster, which is why the Toddy brews on a counter and only the decanter goes cold. The fridge matters afterwards: finished cold brew keeps about two weeks refrigerated, hours at room temperature.

What grind size should I use?

Coarse, coarser than French press. A fine grind passes straight through a mesh filter, leaves silt in the carafe and over-extracts over a 12-hour steep. If your grinder cannot hold a coarse setting, that is the real problem — our grinder guide ranks on exactly that.

Will a cold brew maker fit in a refrigerator door?

Two of the five tell you before you buy. At 94 mm deep and 138 mm wide the Hario is the one shaped like a door-bin object; Toddy publishes 14.5 × 7 × 7 in brewing and 11.75 × 7.5 × 7.5 in for the decanter, a shelf item at either size. Takeya and asobu publish nothing, though both Takeyas can lie on a shelf. Measure your deepest door bin and tallest shelf gap first.
