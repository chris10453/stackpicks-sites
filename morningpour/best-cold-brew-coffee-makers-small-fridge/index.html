Cold brew is the one kind of coffee where the brewer lives in the refrigerator for twelve to twenty-four hours, which makes it the one kind of coffee where the appliance competes with your food for space. That is a real problem in an apartment fridge, and it is the problem almost no review mentions. Four of the five makers below publish a capacity and no dimensions at all.

So this guide is ranked on published size first and coffee second, the same way our small-kitchen drip guide is ranked on cabinet clearance. Measure the tallest gap on a shelf in your fridge, and the depth of the deepest bin in the door, before you read any further.

How to shop for a cold brew coffee maker

Published dimensions, not just capacity
A quart is a quart no matter what shape it comes in, and shape is what decides whether it stands in a door bin, lies on a shelf or has to displace the milk. Exactly one brand here — Hario — publishes width, depth and height. Takeya publishes a capacity and a weight. asobu publishes a capacity that does not match its own retail retail page.
Whether it can lie down
An airtight, leakproof lid turns a tall brewer into a short one, because you can put it on its side on a shelf. Both Takeyas publish a leakproof lid; the Hario and the Toddy do not, so their height is their height.
Ready-to-drink or concentrate
A mesh-filter pitcher makes coffee you pour and drink. The Toddy makes a concentrate you dilute, which means one brew of the biggest object here yields far more finished coffee than one brew of the smallest — the fridge maths is not the same as the capacity maths.
What the filter is, and what it costs to keep using
A permanent stainless mesh costs nothing after purchase and lets some fines through. The Toddy uses a reusable felt filter plus optional paper bags, which is a cleaner cup and a running cost. It is the same trade as paper versus metal in a pour-over.
Glass or plastic
Glass does not hold odours and does not stain; Tritan does not shatter when a wet hand meets a tile floor. Takeya publishes both versions of the same brewer at the same quart capacity, 526 g in glass and 467 g in Tritan, which makes this the cleanest either-or decision on the page.

Questions people ask

How long does cold brew take, and does longer mean stronger?

The published steeps here run from 8 hours (asobu) to 24 (both Takeyas and the Toddy). Longer does extract more, but past about 24 hours you are mostly adding bitterness rather than strength — the usual advice is 12 to 18 hours at room temperature or the full 24 in the fridge, then take the grounds out.

How much coffee do I use for cold brew?

For a ready-to-drink pitcher, around 1 part coffee to 8 parts water by weight — roughly 75 grams of coarse grounds for the Hario's 600 mL. Concentrate is far stronger: Toddy's system takes a full 12 ounces of coffee to 7 cups of water, and you dilute the result.

Does cold brew need to be made in the refrigerator?

No — room temperature works and extracts faster, which is why the Toddy brews on a counter and only the decanter goes cold. The fridge matters for what happens after: finished cold brew keeps about two weeks refrigerated, and hours at room temperature once the grounds are out.

What grind size should I use?

Coarse, coarser than French press. A fine grind will pass straight through a mesh filter and leave silt in the bottom of the carafe, and it over-extracts over a 12-hour steep. If your grinder cannot hold a coarse setting, that is the real problem — our grinder guide ranks on exactly that.

Will a cold brew maker fit in a refrigerator door?

Only the Hario answers this before you buy. At 94 mm deep and 138 mm wide it is the one shaped like a door-bin object; both Takeyas have leakproof lids and can lie on a shelf instead; the Toddy decanter at 11.75 inches tall is a shelf item. Measure the deepest bin in your door and the tallest gap on a shelf, and shop from that.
