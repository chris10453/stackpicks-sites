How to shop for a coffee canister

A capacity you can compare to a bag
Coffee is sold in pounds and 250 g bags; canisters are sold in liters. Fellow and Planetary Design publish a bean weight and Hario publishes both a volume and a gram figure; Ankomn publishes liters only, which leaves the useful number off the box.
How the air actually leaves
Three mechanisms here. Airscape's inner plunger pushes air out through a two-way valve and locks it out. Fellow's and Ankomn's lids pull a partial vacuum. Hario's is a plain silicone gasket that seals but removes nothing — not the same feature under different names.
Whether you have to maintain it
A vacuum leaks. Ankomn tells you to turn the lid every four or five days and Fellow rates its seal at about a week; a plunger lid removes the air once and needs nothing after that.
Light, and whether you can see in
Light stales coffee. Glass and clear plastic let you see the level and let light at the beans; painted steel does neither. Two picks here are opaque, three see-through, and the Fellow is sold both ways.
The body, and what happens in a dishwasher
Ankomn publishes a dishwasher-safe body with a hand-wash lid and a 100,000-turn warranty on the mechanism; Fellow's steel canisters are hand wash. A canister that cannot be washed properly will taste of last month's beans.

Questions people ask

How long do whole beans actually stay fresh?

Two to four weeks from the roast date, and that clock runs whatever you store them in. A canister slows the decline; it does not restart it.

Should I keep coffee in the freezer instead?

Only unopened, in one-use portions, and never with beans you open and close repeatedly — each cycle condenses moisture onto cold beans. None of the canisters here is designed for freezer use.

Does a vacuum canister really do anything?

It removes some of the oxygen and slows oxidation. It is not a hard vacuum: Ankomn asks you to turn the lid every four or five days to keep it, and Fellow rates its seal at around a week unopened. Airscape's plunger is the one mechanism here that does not decay, because it pushes the air out once and locks the lid down on what is left.

Why does 1.2 liters not hold a pound of coffee?

Because roast level changes bean density. Darker roasts have lost more moisture and expanded, so a pound of dark beans takes up more space than a pound of light ones. Fellow publishes this directly — 16 oz of light or medium roast in the 1.2 L, and not a full pound of dark.

Can I store ground coffee in these?

You can, and it will still be stale quickly. Ground coffee exposes many times more surface to air than whole beans and loses its aromatics in days rather than weeks. If you are grinding fresh, a canister is for the beans — our grinder guide covers the other half.

  • Any canister with a one-way degassing valve only — A one-way valve is what a coffee bag has. It lets carbon dioxide out and nothing in, which is the right design for a sealed bag you have not opened yet and does nothing for a jar you open every morning. Airscape's two-way valve is a different part solving a different problem.
  • Mason jars with a hand pump — Cheap and effective while the seal holds. The failure is the pump — a separate object that lives in a drawer, gets lost, and is needed every time the jar is opened.
  • The bag the coffee came in — Not a joke. A one-way valve bag with a tight roll and a clip beats a leaky jar, and for a 250 g bag finished in ten days it is fine. It is what every canister here has to beat.
