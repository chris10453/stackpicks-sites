How to shop for an espresso machine for milk drinks

What the machine does with milk, exactly
This is the whole page. The Bambino Plus textures automatically to a chosen temperature and texture; the Infuser and Barista Express give you a manual wand and a PID; the Dedica has an adjustable frother sleeve with hot-milk and cappuccino settings. Three different amounts of skill required.
Heat-up, because a latte is two operations
A milk drink means pulling a shot and then steaming, which on a single-boiler machine means waiting for the element to climb from brew temperature to steam temperature. Breville publishes three seconds to extraction temperature on the Bambino Plus ThermoJet and a 1650 W element on the Infuser. Two of the five publish no heat-up figure at all.
Temperature control you can see
Breville publishes PID holding 200 °F on the Barista Express and 4 °F increments on the Infuser. Casabrews publishes PID holding 90 to 96 °C, the band SCA Standard 310-2021 specifies. De'Longhi names its heating hardware and publishes no temperature.
Counter width, because the grinder goes next to it
A latte setup is two appliances unless the grinder is inside the machine. The Dedica is 15 cm — under six inches — wide, leaving room for a grinder where nothing else here fits. The Barista Express is 15.9 inches wide and needs no grinder beside it.
Portafilter size and what comes in the box
A 54 mm basket is the Breville standard, with an enormous accessory ecosystem around it. The Bambino Plus also ships a 480 ml stainless milk jug and a razor dosing tool.

Questions people ask

Do I need an automatic milk frother to make a latte at home?

No, but it removes the part that takes longest to learn. Hand-steaming to a glossy microfoam is a two- or three-month skill; the Bambino Plus does it to a chosen temperature and texture on day one. The manual machines here all beat it — eventually, in trained hands.

What temperature should espresso be brewed at?

SCA Standard 310-2021 specifies 90 to 96 °C, which is 194 to 205 °F. Breville publishes 200 °F for the Barista Express under PID and Casabrews publishes the 90 to 96 °C range. De'Longhi publishes a Thermoblock and no temperature — hardware, not a claim about the water.

Is a 20-bar pump better than a 15-bar pump?

No. Espresso is extracted at about 9 bar, and every machine on this page — 15-bar Brevilles and the 20-bar Casabrews alike — regulates down to roughly that. Breville is explicit about it, publishing a 15 bar pump and 9 bar extraction. A higher headline bar number is marketing headroom, not a better shot.

How long does a latte take on these machines?

Breville publishes three seconds to extraction temperature on the Bambino Plus. Add grinding, tamping and about 30 seconds of steaming and one latte is three to four minutes from cold. The fifteen-minute warm-up rule belongs to heavier boilers than these.

Which milk jug size should I use?

The Bambino Plus ships a 480 ml (16 fl oz) stainless jug, right for one 8-ounce latte — a jug wants roughly twice the milk volume so the foam has room. Two drinks at once wants a larger jug, a separate purchase on every machine here.
