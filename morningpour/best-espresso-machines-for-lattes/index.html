How to shop for an espresso machine for milk drinks

What the machine does with milk, exactly
This is the whole page. A Bambino Plus textures automatically to a chosen temperature and texture. An Infuser or a Barista Express gives you a manual wand and a PID to hold the boiler steady. A Dedica has an adjustable frother sleeve with a hot-milk and a cappuccino setting. A Casabrews claims a three-second switch from brew to steam. Those are four different amounts of skill required.
Heat-up, because a latte is two operations
A milk drink means pulling a shot and then steaming, which on a single-boiler machine means waiting for the element to climb from brew temperature to steam temperature. Breville publishes three seconds to extraction temperature on the Bambino Plus ThermoJet and a 1650 W element on the Infuser. De'Longhi publishes a Thermoblock and no time at all.
Temperature control you can see
Breville publishes PID control on the Barista Express, holding 200 °F, and on the Infuser adjustable in 4 °F increments. Casabrews publishes PID holding 90 to 96 °C, which is the band SCA Standard 310-2021 specifies. De'Longhi publishes a Thermoblock and no temperature. A published number is the difference between temperature control and the phrase "temperature control".
Counter width, because the grinder goes next to it
A latte setup is two appliances unless the grinder is inside the machine. The Dedica is 15 cm — under six inches — wide, which leaves room for a grinder on a counter nothing else here fits on. The Barista Express is 15.9 inches wide and needs no grinder beside it. Those are the two ends of the same problem.
Portafilter size and what comes in the box
A 54 mm basket is the Breville standard and the accessory ecosystem around it is enormous. What ships matters too: the Bambino Plus includes a 480 ml stainless milk jug and a razor dosing tool, which are two purchases you do not then make.

Questions people ask

Do I need an automatic milk frother to make a latte at home?

No, but it removes the part that takes longest to learn. Steaming milk by hand to a glossy microfoam is a skill of two or three months for most people; the Bambino Plus does it to a chosen temperature and texture on day one. The three manual machines here all make better milk than the automatic one — eventually, and in trained hands.

What temperature should espresso be brewed at?

SCA Standard 310-2021 specifies 90 to 96 °C, which is 194 to 205 °F. Breville publishes 200 °F for the Barista Express thermocoil under PID; Casabrews publishes a 90 to 96 °C range under PID. De'Longhi publishes a Thermoblock and no temperature at all, which is a description of the hardware rather than a claim about the water.

Is a 20-bar pump better than a 15-bar pump?

No. Espresso is extracted at about 9 bar, and every machine on this page — 15-bar Brevilles and the 20-bar Casabrews alike — regulates down to roughly that. Breville is explicit about it, publishing a 15 bar pump and 9 bar extraction. A higher headline bar number is marketing headroom, not a better shot.

How long does a latte take on these machines?

Breville publishes three seconds to extraction temperature on the Bambino Plus and Casabrews publishes a three-second switch from brewing to steaming. Add grinding, tamping and about 30 seconds of steaming and a single latte is realistically three to four minutes from cold on the fastest machine here. The old rule of a fifteen-minute warm-up belongs to heavier boilers than these.

Which milk jug size should I use?

The Bambino Plus ships with a 480 ml (16 fl oz) stainless jug, which is the right size for one 8-ounce latte — a jug should be roughly twice the milk volume so there is room for the foam to expand. Two drinks at once wants a larger jug, which is a separate purchase on every machine here.
