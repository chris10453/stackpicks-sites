A carrier is bought twice. Once for the drive to a clinic, where what matters is whether a frightened cat can be got in and out without a wrestling match, and once for a flight, where what matters is one number the airline cares about and your cat does not.

Almost nobody tests that number. All six makers here print exterior dimensions and five print a cat weight, but only two of those weights come from an independent crash test; the rest are the factory's own figure, and one maker prints no limit on its own page at all. This guide sorts them by what the maker is willing to stand behind, because at the gate and on the exam table that is all you have.

How to shop for a cat carrier

Published exterior dimensions
Delta's own pet page says the maximum in-cabin kennel size is set by the aircraft, and recommends a soft-sided carrier of 18 by 11 by 11 inches because that fits most types. You cannot check a carrier against that unless the maker prints its own figures.
An independent weight rating
Center for Pet Safety certification means dynamic crash testing to a stated animal weight, not a marketing team calling something sturdy. Two carriers here have it; the rest publish a factory figure, or nothing.
How the cat gets in
A top-loading carrier lets you lower a cat in rather than post it through a doorway, which for a cat that plants all four feet at the threshold is the difference between a two-minute departure and a twenty-minute one.
Whether it lives out in the room
A carrier stored in a closet is a trap that appears on vet day. One that doubles as a bed is furniture the cat already sleeps in.

Questions people ask

What size cat carrier do airlines allow?

There is no single figure. Delta's pet travel page says maximum in-cabin kennel dimensions are determined by the aircraft, and recommends a soft-sided kennel with maximum dimensions of 18 by 11 by 11 inches since that fits most types. It also requires the kennel to fit fully under the seat in front of you and to have ventilation on three sides for domestic travel, four for international. Check your own airline and aircraft before you buy.

Is a hard or soft carrier better for the vet?

Hard, if the cat resists. The Petmate kennel here opens from the top and its two halves unclip, so a cat can be examined in the bottom half rather than pulled through a 10 inch door. Soft carriers with a top zip are the next best thing. The worst is a front-door-only box of any material.

How heavy a cat can these carriers take?

Published limits run from 10 pounds on the Petmate kennel to 25 on the Fat Cat backpack, with the Odyssey at 20. Only the two Sleepypod carriers carry an independent figure — 15 pounds for the Mobile Pet Bed and 18 for the Air, both certified by the Center for Pet Safety. Rabbitgoo names no limit on its own store at all.

Should the carrier go on the car seat?

Only if it is built for that. Sleepypod's two carriers were dynamically crash-tested to a child-restraint standard and are certified to 15 and 18 pounds. Nothing else here makes a restraint claim, so those carriers belong on the floor behind a front seat, where they cannot fall far.
