Cat bowls are sold on looks and returned on geometry. The two numbers that decide whether a cat finishes its food are the width of the dish, which decides whether the whiskers touch the sides, and the depth, which decides whether the cat has to put its face into a well.

Every product here publishes both numbers, which is the reason these five made the page and a lot of better-known bowls did not. The rest of the decision is material and washing: stainless steel and ceramic are the two surfaces that go through a dishwasher and do not hold a film, and the difference between them is mostly the weight of the thing on a tile floor.

How to shop for a cat bowl

Published width and depth
A shallow dish of about five to six inches across lets a cat eat without its whiskers brushing the sides. Every bowl in this comparison publishes its measurements in inches; the brands that publish only "cat sized" are in the rejects.
Material you can actually clean
Ceramic and 304 stainless steel wash at dishwasher temperatures and do not develop the scratched film that plastic does. Scratches hold bacteria and old oils, which is the ordinary explanation for a cat that stops using a bowl it used last month.
Height, and whether it is honest about it
A raised dish keeps the neck straighter and is easier for an older cat. It also raises the height a knocked bowl falls from. Two of these publish the stand height; the rest sit on the floor and say so.
Whether it looks like kitchenware
Feeding stations live in the part of a small flat visitors see. A white ceramic dish on a steel stand passes as kitchen equipment. A plastic double bowl in a printed pattern does not.

Questions people ask

What size should a cat bowl be?

Wide and shallow. The dishes here run from 4.8 to 6.2 inches across with wells of about one to two inches deep, which lets an average cat eat without its whiskers touching the sides. If your cat pulls food out of the bowl and eats it off the floor, the bowl is too narrow or too deep.

Are raised cat bowls better?

They are easier on an older or arthritic cat, and they help a cat that eats fast and brings food back. The raised models here sit at 3.2 inches on the Tuft + Paw stand and 3.9 inches tall on the Catit PIXI. For a young cat with no issues a floor-level shallow dish is fine.

Ceramic or stainless steel?

Both go in the dishwasher and neither develops the scratched film that plastic does. Ceramic is heavier so it stays put; steel is unbreakable and lighter, which means it needs a rubber ring or skid feet. Choose on whether the bowl gets pushed around or dropped in your house.

Should food and water be in the same station?

Cats generally drink more when water is away from the food. The double dish here is convenient, but veterinary environmental guidance asks for feeding and drinking areas in separate locations, so a fountain or water bowl on the other side of the room is the better setup — our fountain guide covers the quiet ones.

How often should a cat bowl be washed?

Every day for wet food, which is why the two Catit dishes ship with a spare insert. A rinse is not a wash; the film that builds on a bowl is what puts a cat off a dish it liked last week.
