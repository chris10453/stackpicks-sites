Most cat-furniture articles skip what the sources actually say. The ASPCA says to remove covers and liners from all litter boxes, and lists an uncomfortable hood among the reasons cats stop using one. Ohio State says cats generally prefer large uncovered boxes and that covers concentrate odours. Cornell puts it in a line: covers hold odours in.

So why recommend enclosures at all? Because in a 600-square-foot apartment the alternative is not a quiet spare room — it is an open pan beside the sofa, which fails the other half of the same guidance: quiet, accessible, away from food and traffic. An enclosure buys the right location at the cost of airflow, and that trade only works if you scoop daily and the cabinet ventilates.

How to shop for an enclosed litter box

Doorway size
The biggest difference between the two cabinets here. Yaheetech publishes 6.5 x 8 inches; Feandrea publishes no opening figure at all, on a 31.5-inch cabinet with a full barn-door front.
Ventilation
Covers concentrate odour, so back-panel holes and doors that do not seal are what keep an enclosure from becoming a box of ammonia. Yaheetech publishes seven vent holes; the Feandrea's larger volume does some of the same work.
Interior volume per cat
A cat has to stand up and turn around inside, and Ohio State's rule for a box applies to the cabinet around it. 19.8 x 19.8 x 18.3 inches against 15.7 x 19.6 x 17.6 is a real difference for a heavy cat.
How fast you can scoop it
Everything about an enclosure gets worse if scooping is a chore. Both open from the front; the Feandrea's double barn doors beat a single hinged door if the cabinet sits in a corner.

Questions people ask

Do enclosed litter boxes smell worse?

They concentrate it where the cat is. Ohio State states that covered boxes concentrate odours more than uncovered ones, and Cornell says covers hold odours in. You smell less; the cat smells more. Vent holes and daily scooping are what make that trade fair.

Will a cat refuse to use an enclosure?

Some do. The ASPCA lists an uncomfortable hood or liner among the common causes of a cat eliminating outside the box. The test costs nothing: put the enclosure up, keep the open pan for two weeks, and let the cat vote.

How big does the doorway need to be?

Bigger than 6.5 inches for a large cat. Published openings here run from 6.5 x 8 inches to a 10.6-inch cat hole, and Cornell's guidance that larger cats need bigger boxes applies to the opening too. If your cat pauses at the threshold, it is too small.

How often do I need to scoop an enclosed box?

The ASPCA's baseline is scooping at least once a day and a full clean weekly with warm water and unscented soap, replacing the litter. In an enclosure I treat that as a floor, not a target, because the box is inside a cupboard in your living room.

How many boxes should I have if I use an enclosure?

The ASPCA's rule does not change: one per cat plus one extra. Two cats means three boxes. The realistic version in a small apartment is one enclosure in the living area and a plain open pan somewhere quieter — which also gives a cat that dislikes covers a legitimate alternative.
