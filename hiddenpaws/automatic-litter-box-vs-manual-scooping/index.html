How to shop for a litter box

Floor area, not price
The robot needs 22 by 27 inches of floor plus clearance to open, and it stands 29.5 inches tall, which is counter height. The hooded pan is 17.5 by 20 inches and 16.5 tall. In a bathroom where the box lives beside the basin, that difference decides the argument before money does.
The entry your cat will accept
Whisker publishes a 15.75 inch square entryway and a step up into the globe. The hooded pan has a front door at floor level. A kitten, a senior cat or a cat with arthritis has a view about this, and the cat's view is final.
What happens when you are away
The robot sifts after each use and drops waste into a sealed drawer that Whisker says goes up to eight days with one cat. A manual box with no scooping is a manual box a cat may stop using by day two.
What happens when the power goes off
An automatic box is an appliance, and an appliance fails. Whisker's own guidance for kittens under three pounds is to run the unit unplugged in semi-automatic mode, which is a useful reminder that the fallback is scooping it yourself.
Total cost over two years
The robot is a single large purchase plus liners and litter. The pan is a small purchase plus litter and a scoop. Neither is the wrong answer, but only one of them still costs the same when you move flat.

Questions people ask

How much space does an automatic litter box need?

More than people expect. Whisker publishes 22 inches wide, 27 deep and 29.5 tall for the Litter-Robot 4, plus clearance for the waste drawer. The hooded pan here is 17.5 by 20 by 16.5 inches. Mark the larger footprint on the floor with tape before you order anything.

Will my cat use an automatic litter box?

Most do, given a transition. The entryway is 15.75 inches square and there is a step up into the globe, so the cats that struggle are kittens, very old cats and cats with joint problems. Whisker's advice for kittens under three pounds is to run the unit unplugged until they are bigger.

Is an automatic box worth the money?

It is worth it if the alternative is an unscooped box. The robot sifts after every use and seals the waste in a drawer that runs up to eight days with one cat, which is a different level of consistency from a pan scooped when someone remembers. If you already scoop daily, the pan is the better value by a wide margin.

Do automatic boxes work with any litter?

They are built around clumping litter, because the mechanism sifts clumps into the drawer. Non-clumping paper or crystal litter does not work in a sifting box — see our clumping versus non-clumping guide for which one your cat should be on.

How many litter boxes do two cats need?

The standard veterinary guidance is one box per cat plus one, in separate locations. That applies to automatic boxes too, which is the argument people forget when pricing one — two cats in a flat want more than a single machine in a single corner.

Also considered

  • Open pans with no hood — Cheaper again, and they scatter litter and show the contents from across a studio flat. An open pan is the right answer for a cat that refuses enclosures, and that is a cat problem rather than a design preference.
  • Automatic boxes with no published dimensions — Several machines in this category publish a photograph and a price. An appliance that takes up a corner of a small flat and has no published footprint or entry size cannot be compared or planned for.
  • Self-cleaning boxes using proprietary trays — Crystal-tray systems tie the running cost to one supplier's refills. It is a real ongoing cost that the sticker price hides, and it makes the litter choice somebody else's.
