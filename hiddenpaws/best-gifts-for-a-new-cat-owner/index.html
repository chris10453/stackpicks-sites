The rule for buying a gift for someone else's cat is that you cannot measure the cat or the flat. That rules out beds, carriers, litter furniture and anything sized to a doorway, and it leaves toys, food gear, and the two or three things every new owner ends up buying anyway.

Everything here is under the same constraint: it works without knowing the cat's weight, the size of the sofa or which room the litter box is in. Two of the six are adjustable across a whole size range, and the rest are one-size objects.

How to shop for a gift for a new cat owner

Works without measuring anything
A gift that needs the cat's chest girth or the flat's floor plan becomes a return. Adjustable or one-size items are what survive being bought by someone who has met the cat twice.
Useful in the first month
New owners buy a bed, a bowl and a litter box in the first week and then discover what is missing. The gaps are enrichment, travel gear and the small things nobody thinks of, which is where these six sit.
Something they would not buy themselves
A gift that duplicates what is already in the flat is a waste. Nobody buys themselves a decent cat blanket or a travel bowl until the first road trip.
Good-looking enough to leave out
A first-time owner in a small flat is already negotiating between the cat and the room. A gift that looks like equipment loses that negotiation and lives in a cupboard.

Questions people ask

What do you buy someone who just got a cat?

Something that does not need the cat's measurements. A food puzzle, a toy, a travel bowl or a blanket all work without knowing the cat's weight or the flat's layout. Beds, carriers, litter furniture and harnesses all depend on a number you probably do not have.

Is it safe to give a collar as a gift?

Only a breakaway one. The collar here opens under force, which is what prevents a cat being caught by its neck on a branch or a handle. A fixed buckle collar on a cat that goes outdoors is a hazard, not a gift.

What should you not give a new cat owner?

Food, litter and anything sized. A cat's diet is often set by a vet, litter type is a live argument in the house, and a bed sized wrong gets returned. Toys, enrichment and travel gear avoid all three problems.

Which of these works for a kitten?

The play circuit and the collar. The circuit rewards chasing, which is what a kitten does all day, and the collar adjusts down to 8 inches. The MEGA puzzle also works for a kitten once it is eating solid food, using the easiest openings.

How much should a cat gift cost?

The useful range here runs from a collar you could buy with pocket change to a designer blanket. The most-given-and-used answer is the middle of the page — a puzzle or a track that changes the cat's day rather than an object that sits in the room.

Also considered

  • Cat beds — The classic gift and the classic mistake. The cat chooses its own sleeping place and a bed that does not match the spot the cat has already picked gets ignored. A blanket goes on whatever the cat has chosen.
  • Gift baskets of treats — Cat diets are often prescribed and new owners are usually mid-transition between foods. A basket of unknown treats is a gift the owner has to throw away quietly.
  • Automatic laser toys — They give a chase with no catch, and they stop being interesting once the cat learns the pattern. A puzzle the cat can actually win is a better use of the same money.
  • Anything that needs the flat measured — Litter furniture, cat trees, window perches and wall shelves are all good purchases and all bad gifts, because every one of them depends on a measurement only the owner has. Send them our litter furniture guide instead and let them pick.
