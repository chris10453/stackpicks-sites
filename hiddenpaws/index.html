Cat gear that fits a real apartment — a buying guide for small homes

The problem with cat furniture is not that it is expensive. It is that it is beige, it is carpeted, and it announces itself from the doorway of a 700-square-foot apartment. The gear on this site does the cat's job without redecorating your home around a litter pan.

Every guide starts from a measurement — the interior width of a cabinet, the height of a door opening, the diameter of a sisal post — because in a small apartment the measurement is the whole decision. How Hidden Paws picks

Each guide names a winner in the first sixty words, then explains who should buy something else instead. Specifications are quoted from manufacturer pages and manuals, not from retailer bullet points, and every guide lists those sources at the bottom. Where a brand does not publish a number we need, the guide says the number is unpublished rather than inventing one.

We never print prices or star ratings. Prices change weekly and a stale number is worse than none; the "Check price" buttons send you to the retailer to see today's figure. Awards are written around a buyer — "best for a hallway", "best for two cats" — because a single "best" is useless when the constraint is your floor plan.
