This is a comparison where one brand publishes what the rest of the category hides. Modkat prints the top-entry opening at 9.5 inches, the front entrance at 8.25 by 9.5 inches, the height of that entrance from the floor at 6.75 inches, and a lid capacity for both. Four numbers, and they settle the argument better than any amount of prose about litter tracking.

What neither design changes is the welfare advice underneath. The ASPCA's position on covers is to remove them, and Ohio State's Indoor Pet Initiative says cats generally prefer large uncovered boxes. A lidded box of either kind is a compromise you make for your floor, and the way to make it a fair one is to keep the old open pan in the flat for two weeks and let the cat vote.

How to shop for a top-entry litter box

Step height at the entrance
The single number that predicts whether an older cat keeps using the box. A front entrance 6.75 inches from the floor is a step; the wall of a top-entry box is a jump onto a lid at fifteen inches. Cornell's feline behavior guidance notes that kittens and elderly cats need low sides, and a high entrance is the same obstacle as a high wall.
Opening size against the cat's chest
A 9.5-inch top hole and an 8.25 by 9.5-inch front doorway are near-identical in width, so a broad-chested cat is equally squeezed by both. The difference is that a cat entering from above drops through; a cat entering from the front has to duck and walk.
Tracking
Top entry exists for this. The lid is the walk-off surface, and litter kicked up inside hits a roof instead of a room. The Modkat Flip answers with a front step designed to contain litter, which is a partial fix rather than the same fix.
Whether it keeps a dog out
Modkat says its top-entry box keeps most dogs out, which is a real feature in a two-species flat and something no front-entry box can claim. A curved corridor in a litter cabinet is the other way to solve it.
Lid capacity
These lids get stood on, and by more than the cat. Modkat publishes 30 lb for the top-entry lid and 24 lb for the Flip's magnetic lid, which tells you which one tolerates being a shelf and which one does not.
How you scoop it
A swivel lid that drops stray litter back inside is a different weekly experience from a hinged front lid you lift. Both Modkat designs open properly; most marketplace hoods do not.

The verdict

Top entry if your cat is under about 12 lb, agile, and your problem is litter on the floor or a dog in the box. Front entry if your cat is heavy, elderly, short-legged or hesitant, because 6.75 inches is a step and fifteen inches is a decision. Those are the two sentences.

And the third sentence, which the welfare sources insist on: neither of these is what a cat would choose. Keep the open pan for a fortnight after the new box arrives, put both where the guidance says — quiet, accessible, away from food and noisy appliances — and watch which one gets used before you get rid of anything.

Questions people ask

Do top-entry litter boxes really reduce tracking?

Yes, and the mechanism is geometric rather than magic. Litter kicked toward a wall hits a roof, and the cat then walks out across that roof, leaving most of what it is carrying on the lid. The Modkat lid is explicitly described as doubling as a walk-off mat.

Are top-entry litter boxes bad for older cats?

They are bad for any cat that cannot comfortably jump onto a fifteen-inch cube and drop ten inches inside. Cornell's feline behavior guidance notes that kittens and elderly cats need low sides, and a top entry is the highest side of all. If your cat has started pausing before it jumps onto the sofa, this is not the box.

What size should the entry hole be?

Wider than your cat's chest, with room to spare. The two published openings here — 9.5 inches across on the top-entry box and 8.25 by 9.5 inches at the front of the Flip — are near-identical in width, so the difference between the designs is the approach rather than the squeeze.

Will a top-entry box keep the dog out of the litter?

Mostly. Modkat claims it keeps most dogs out, which is the honest version of the claim, and a determined small dog is still a determined small dog. The other answer is an enclosure with a turned corridor, covered in the hidden litter furniture guide.

Does a lidded box make the smell worse?

It concentrates it. Ohio State's Indoor Pet Initiative states that covered boxes concentrate odors more than uncovered ones, and Cornell makes the same point. That is an argument for scooping daily rather than an argument against lids, but it is the reason the welfare guidance keeps recommending open pans.
