A litter mat is the cheapest thing on this site and the one most likely to be bought twice. The first one is too small, sits half under the box, and moves the tracking line eighteen inches instead of stopping it. The second one is the size you should have bought.

There are three constructions here and they fail differently: honeycomb mats drop litter into a sealed lower layer you pour out, ridged fabric mats absorb urine as well as catching grit, and silicone mats wipe clean but slide. The right one depends on whether your problem is tracking, spraying, or both.

How to shop for a litter mat

Published size, in inches
The only figure that separates a mat that works from a mat that moves the problem. A cat leaves the box, takes two to four strides, and the litter comes off those strides — so the mat has to cover the strides, not the doorway.
Construction
Honeycomb and coiled mesh trap grit in a lower layer. Ridged fabric catches grit in grooves and absorbs liquid. Silicone catches in channels and wipes. They solve different halves of the same mess.
How you empty it
A two-layer mat you can fold and pour back into the box takes ten seconds. A mat you have to carry to a bin, or vacuum, is a mat that gets emptied monthly instead of weekly.
Whether it copes with urine
Side-peeing cats and enclosure users spill. An absorbent, urine-proof mat turns that from a floor repair into a wash; a mesh mat lets it straight through to the floorboards.

Questions people ask

What size litter mat do I actually need?

Big enough for two to four strides out of the box. In practice that is 24 by 15 inches at the very least for a small open pan, 30 by 20 inches for most setups, and 35 to 47 inches long in front of an enclosure where the cat exits through a single door every time.

Do honeycomb litter mats really work better?

They work differently. The honeycomb holes let grit fall into a sealed lower layer instead of holding it on the surface, so the litter you catch stays caught until you fold the mat and pour it back. A single-layer textured mat holds the same grit where the next cat can kick it out.

Will a litter mat stop a cat that pees over the side?

Only an absorbent one will. Gorilla Grip's coiled mesh and honeycomb mats pass liquid straight through to the floor; the Drymate mats are built around an absorbent top layer on a waterproof backing, which is why they are the picks for that particular cat.

How do you clean a litter mat?

It depends on the construction, and the makers are specific. Gorilla Grip says hand wash, rinse and air dry only. Drymate says hose, power wash, or machine wash separately and hang to dry, and do not bleach. Catit's mat is a shake-and-wipe job. None of them are dishwasher or tumble dryer items.
