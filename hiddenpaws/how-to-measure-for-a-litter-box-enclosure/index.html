How to measure for a litter box enclosure — a four-minute guide with a tape

By Chris Y. · Two cats, one-bedroom apartment; measures the inside of everything Updated September 11, 2026

Almost every returned litter box cabinet is a measuring failure, not a taste failure. The cabinet arrives, the pan does not go in, or it goes in and the cat will not, and back it goes at your expense with 35 lb of particleboard to repack.

Four minutes and a tape prevent it. You need three numbers before you shop — the footprint of the pan you already own, its height plus the headroom your cat needs above it, and the size of the hole the cat has to walk through — and then you compare them against the interior dimensions a manufacturer publishes, never the exterior ones.

This page shows how to take those three numbers, gives you the published interiors and doorways of the cabinets we recommend so you can check yours against real figures, and ends with the one-line rule for the cabinet that is about to tempt you on a marketplace page with no specification sheet at all.

Questions people ask

Should a litter box really be 1.5 times the length of my cat?

That rule is printed by manufacturers — Petmate puts it on a sizing graphic on its own product page — but I could not find it stated by a veterinary organization. The vet sources stick to a functional rule instead: Ohio State says the box should be large enough for your cat to stand up on all fours and turn around in, and Cornell says larger cats need bigger boxes. Use the functional test; treat 1.5 times as a shopping shortcut, not a standard.

How much clearance does a litter pan need inside a cabinet?

Two inches on each side of the footprint and about eight inches above an open pan's rim. A 17.5 by 20-inch pan therefore needs roughly a 21.5 by 24-inch interior, which is larger than three of the six cabinets in our guide.

How deep should the litter be, and does that change the height I need?

The ASPCA says cats usually prefer one to two inches of litter, so the litter itself adds barely an inch to your height calculation — but it does add weight when wet, which matters for the cabinet's published interior load rating rather than for clearance.

What if the brand does not publish an interior dimension?

Treat that as a measurement you will take yourself on arrival, and keep the packaging until you have. Three of the products most recommended in this category publish no interior dimension at all, and two of them have no working manufacturer website to ask.

Can I fit two litter boxes in one enclosure?

Only in the divided-bench style, and only if both sections clear your pan. Yaheetech's divided cabinet publishes an 18.6 by 17.7-inch left section and an 8-inch-wide right section, which is a box and a storage cupboard rather than two boxes. For two cats the ASPCA wants three boxes anyway, so plan on more than one piece of furniture.

Does the enclosure need vent holes?

Yes, and preferably more than a token few. Ohio State states that covered boxes concentrate odours more than uncovered ones, and Cornell says covers hold odours in. Cabinets in our guide publish seven to nine vent holes in the back panel; a sealed cabinet with a single doorway is a worse place to be a cat.

Number 1: your pan's footprint, plus two inches a side

Measure the widest points of your litter pan, not the base. Most pans flare, so the top rim is two to three inches wider than the bottom, and the rim is what has to clear the cabinet walls. Write down length and width in inches.

Then add two inches to each side. That clearance is not decoration: it is the room you need to lift the pan out at an angle, to slide a litter mat alongside it, and to stop the pan wedging against a wall panel that has swollen slightly in a humid month. A pan measuring 17.5 by 20 inches therefore wants an interior of about 21.5 by 24 inches — which is larger than several cabinets sold specifically to hold it.

Number 2: height, plus headroom the cat can stand in

For an open pan, measure from the floor to the top of the rim and add at least eight inches of clear air above it. Ohio State's Indoor Pet Initiative sets the standard for the box itself — as a rule the litter box should be large enough for your cat to stand up on all fours and turn around in — and inside a cabinet that rule applies to the cabinet, because the cabinet is now the box's ceiling.

For a hooded pan, the hood height is your number: a 16.5-inch hood needs an interior of at least 17 inches before the lid can be lifted at all, and more if you want to take the hood off without removing the whole unit from the cupboard. This is why so many cabinets with an 18-inch interior height are, in practice, open-pan-only.

Cabinet interiors in this category cluster between 17.6 and 19 inches of height. If your pan plus headroom exceeds that, you are shopping for a taller cabinet, not a nicer one.

Number 3: the doorway, measured against your actual cat

This is the number brands hide and buyers forget. Measure your cat's shoulder height while it is standing — floor to the top of the shoulder blades — and its chest width at the widest point. A cat will fold through a hole smaller than that; the question is whether it will keep choosing to.

Published doorways vary enormously. Yaheetech publishes a 6.5 by 8-inch opening on its 19-inch side-table cabinet and a 10.6-inch-high cat hole on a larger model. Most other brands, including Feandrea and Hzuaneri, publish no opening dimension at all, which means you are measuring it on arrival with the return window running.

Two rules of thumb from the guidance, not from marketing: Cornell's Feline Health Center notes that larger cats need bigger boxes, and that kittens and elderly cats need low sides — a high threshold at the doorway is the same obstacle as a high box wall. And if your cat pauses at the opening rather than walking straight through, the doorway is too small, whatever the tape says.

The dividers are structural — read the small print

Several cabinets get their large interior only when you remove an internal panel, and that panel is holding the top up. Feandrea states it plainly on its own page: with the divider in place the top is rated to 132 lb, and with it removed that rating falls to 22 lb. Decide which cabinet you are building before you assemble it, because a 22 lb top is a plant and a photo frame, not a lamp, a stack of books and a cat.

Where it goes matters as much as what fits

The ASPCA's placement guidance is worth taking literally in a small home: put boxes in accessible locations away from high-traffic areas and away from anywhere a cat could feel trapped, keep them away from loud appliances such as a washer and dryer, and do not put the food bowls next to the box. It also recommends one box per cat plus one extra, and a box on each level in a multistory home.

In a one-bedroom apartment that usually resolves to one enclosure somewhere the household walks past but does not sit, and a second box — often a plain open pan — somewhere quieter. That second box is also your insurance policy if the cat turns out to dislike the enclosure.

If you use a self-cleaning box

Measure the machine, not the pan, and include the height of its travel. A Litter-Robot 4 has a published footprint of 22 by 27 inches and stands 29.5 inches high with a 15.75-inch square entryway. No cabinet interior in our guide comes close, so the honest answer for that machine is a cabinet built and sold specifically for self-cleaning boxes — and at the time of writing the only one we found that advertises the fit publishes no dimensions at all, which makes it unbuyable on evidence.

One more number nobody prints: replacement interval

Petmate recommends replacing a cat's litter box every 12 months, on the grounds that odours penetrate plastic and resins over time. Whether or not you follow that schedule, it is a useful reminder that the pan inside a beautiful cabinet is a consumable — and that a cabinet whose interior only just fits today's pan will not fit the slightly larger one you buy next year.

The four-minute checklist

  1. Pan footprint at the widest point, plus two inches each side. That is your minimum interior length and width.
  2. Pan height plus eight inches of headroom, or the full hood height plus lifting room. That is your minimum interior height.
  3. Cat's shoulder height and chest width. Compare against the published doorway; if the brand publishes none, decide whether you are willing to find out on arrival.
  4. Check the load rating you will actually have — with or without the divider you plan to remove.
  5. Measure the spot in the room, including the door swing. A 31.5-inch cabinet with barn doors needs clearance in front of it that a 19-inch side table does not.
