Clumping vs non-clumping cat litter (2026), settled on scooping and dust

By Chris Y. · Two cats, one-bedroom apartment; measures the inside of everything Updated September 12, 2026

The short version — buy clumping unless the cat or a person in the house has a respiratory problem. Dr. Elsey's Ultra is the one to get for most flats because a hard clump can be lifted out whole, which is what keeps a box in a small apartment from smelling. ökocat's Dust Free paper pellet litter is the one to get when dust is the deciding factor, because it is reclaimed paper rather than clay and the maker aims it at cats with respiratory ailments.

The two types ask for different work. Clumping clay turns urine into a solid lump you scoop daily and top up weekly, and the litter in the box is largely the litter you bought a month ago. Non-clumping absorbs liquid down into the bed, which you scoop for solids daily and then replace entirely on a schedule.

That difference decides everything else: how much you carry home, how the box smells on day five, how much dust settles on a dark floor, and how much of a 700 square foot flat smells of cat when the windows are shut.

How to shop for cat litter

What happens to the urine
Dr. Elsey's describes a hard clump that prevents moisture reaching the bottom of the pan and will not break apart, so the whole deposit leaves the box. ökocat's paper pellets absorb the liquid from the bottom of the pan and expand, so you remove the swollen pellets instead. One is surgery, the other is maintenance.
Dust
Dr. Elsey's publishes a 99 percent dust-free claim for a clay litter. ökocat calls its pellets virtually dust-free and says the litter is ideal for cats with respiratory ailments and homes with allergies. Neither is a measurement, but one is a number and one is an adjective, and in a flat where the box is eight feet from where you sleep this is the first thing to read.
The replacement schedule
ökocat says that with regular scooping the litter can last up to seven weeks, and separately advises replacing the litter weekly. Clumping clay is topped up as it is scooped and fully changed far less often. Read both as the maker's own words, not as a promise about your cat.
What you carry home
A 40 pound bag of clay lasts a long time and has to be carried up the stairs. An 8 pound bag of paper pellets is a different kind of purchase — lighter, more often, and easier in a building with no lift.
Tracking
Dr. Elsey's sells heavy non-tracking granules with medium grain clay; ökocat's pellets are large and low-tracking by size. Both are better than lightweight clay, which is the type that ends up in the hallway. A mat under the box does more than either, and our litter mat guide covers the ones that actually trap granules.

Not the right fit if someone in the house has asthma, or the cat is recovering from surgery — the paper pellets exist for exactly that.

Questions people ask

Which litter is better for a small apartment?

Clumping, in almost every case. Removing a solid clump takes the ammonia out of the room entirely, which is what matters when the box is in a bathroom eight feet from the sofa. Non-clumping leaves the liquid in the bed until the pellets are removed or the box is changed.

Is clumping litter safe for kittens?

The usual veterinary caution is for very young kittens that eat litter, where a non-clumping paper or pellet litter is the safer choice until the habit passes. That is one of the standard reasons ökocat's paper pellets exist, along with post-surgical paws and respiratory problems.

How often do you change each type?

Clumping clay is scooped daily and topped up, with a full change every few weeks. ökocat's own page says the litter can last up to seven weeks with regular scooping and also advises replacing it weekly, so plan on a weekly change and treat seven weeks as the best case with one very tidy cat.

Does non-clumping litter smell worse?

On day two, yes, because the urine is still in the box. Both makers rely on odor control rather than fragrance — Dr. Elsey's controls odor without perfumes and ökocat fortifies its paper fiber with plant-based odor control to suppress ammonia. Scooping frequency matters more than the material.

What about dust?

Dr. Elsey's publishes a 99 percent dust-free claim, which is good for clay and still means some dust. ökocat calls its pellets virtually dust-free and aims the product at cats with respiratory ailments and homes with allergies. Neither maker publishes a measured airborne figure, so read the wording rather than the word dust-free on the front.

Also considered

  • Scented clumping litters — Fragrance is added for the owner and is one of the routine explanations for a cat that stops using its box. Both litters here are unscented, which is the right default.
  • Lightweight clay litters — Lighter granules travel further on paws, which is the most common complaint about clay in a flat with hard floors. Heavy granules and pellets both track less.
  • Crystal silica litters — They absorb well and they are the type most often refused by cats with sensitive paws, which is the same group of cats a paper pellet litter is bought for. Worth a try only if the cat already likes them.
  • Tofu and corn clumping litters — Good products in many homes and out of scope for this comparison, which is about the two mainstream mechanisms — a litter that binds urine and one that absorbs it.
